## What the framework reveals about this segment

Moore's framework is brutal here: do not sell to "regulated enterprises." Sell to one beachhead whose pain is so acute they will tolerate the discomfort of being early. That niche in 2026 is AI Model Risk & Governance leads at Tier-2 BFSI and regional healthcare payers — institutions large enough to face the new AI-use auditor by mid-2027 but too small to have a McKinsey-built program in place. Tier-1 banks already have homegrown GRC scaffolding; startups are below the regulatory line; pharma R&D moves on 7-year cycles. The Tier-2 governance lead has a board mandate, a vendor budget, and no defensible answer to "show me your AI decision provenance." That single persona, in roughly 800 US/EU institutions, is the chasm-crossing beachhead. When they succeed, pragmatist Tier-1s pull team-sdk across — but only after the early adopters have produced audit-survived reference stories.

## Honest weaknesses of this alternative

This combination is awkward in two specific ways. First, team-sdk's core value proposition is "compound team judgment over time" — but the Tier-2 governance buyer is buying *evidence for an auditor*, not judgment compounding. The substrate has to be sold as a control framework first and a learning system second, which inverts the product's own self-description and risks team-sdk becoming a GRC vendor in the market's eyes. Second, Moore demands ruthless beachhead focus; committing here for 18 months means under-investing in the design-led / engineering-team segments where the product's soul actually fits better. The chasm gets crossed, but possibly into a market that reshapes the product into something its principles did not intend.
