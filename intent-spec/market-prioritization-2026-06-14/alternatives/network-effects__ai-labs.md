# Alternative: Network Effects + Reed/Metcalfe × AI labs / research orgs

**Framework:** Network Effects + Reed/Metcalfe
**Segment:** AI labs / research orgs
**Generated:** 2026-06-14

## The market direction in one sentence

Frontier-adjacent AI labs and research orgs adopt team-sdk as a federated substrate so their loop patterns, red-team critiques, and provenance chains cross-pollinate into a shared corpus that compounds Reed-style across the network.

## What the framework reveals about this segment

Metcalfe's law says value scales with n^2 (pairwise connections); Reed's law says it scales with 2^n once subgroups can form. AI labs are the rare segment where both apply non-trivially: researchers already trade critiques, share eval harnesses, and co-author across institutions. The dimension where team-sdk gets more valuable as more labs adopt it is not the agent personas — those are local — but the **Section 32 Critique-Revise/Red-Team patterns, the Halloway Ratchet provenance chains, and the `_signals.jsonl` friction taxonomies**. Each lab that joins contributes new loop patterns and adversarial probes; each subgroup (alignment labs, mech-interp labs, post-training labs) can form Reed-style coalitions that share Layer B domain context without leaking Layer A constraints. The lens reveals team-sdk's natural shape here is a **federated corpus protocol**, not a SaaS.

## The team-sdk value proposition for this segment, via this framework

The configuration that lands hardest: a **labs-mode profile** where Layer A stays sovereign per lab, but Layer B "evidence-shaped" domain context — specifically Section 32 critique transcripts, Section 24 Ratchet provenance, and the cross-cadence retrospective harvest — can be **selectively federated** under signed, citable units. A new lab joining gets immediate access to canonical critique patterns ("how Anthropic's red-team flagged X-class drift," "how DeepMind's mech-interp loop catches Y"). They contribute back their own loop signals. The §18 context request protocol becomes a cross-org primitive: lab A's PM can request, with provenance, lab B's published friction signals on RLHF eval design. The agents are the perspectives; the Bus becomes a federated peer-review fabric.

## GTM motion implied

**Community-led, anchored by one or two prestige design partners.** This segment is allergic to sales motions and responds to peer adoption and published artifacts. The motion: open-source the labs-mode profile, get one credible lab (Anthropic, MATS, Apollo, METR) to publish a paper or blog citing team-sdk as their internal substrate, then let citation-chain prestige drive the next 10 labs. Conferences (NeurIPS, ICML workshops, AI Safety Camp) are the distribution channel. The framework demands this: Reed-style value only ignites once 8-10 nodes are live and subgroups can form, so community-led is the only motion that gets you past the cold-start.

## Monetization shape

**Hybrid: open-core substrate + paid federation/governance tier.** Labs run the substrate free; paid tier is signed provenance registry, cross-org access control, and corpus hosting.

## Honest weaknesses of this alternative

The combo is real but fragile. Reed's law assumes subgroups *want* to form — but frontier labs are competitive on capability work and only collaborative on safety. The federation surface narrows to alignment/eval/interp work, which is a small slice of total lab activity. Worse, the network effect cuts the other way pre-tipping-point: until 8-10 labs federate, a single lab gets only the local substrate value (which competes against bespoke internal tooling already built). Willingness to pay is moderate and procurement cycles are slow even for free tools. The framework wants n^2 or 2^n; the segment realistically delivers linear adoption for 12-18 months. There is also a non-trivial risk that one prestige lab's adoption *prevents* rivals from adopting — anti-network effect via brand association.
