# Alternative: Network Effects + Reed/Metcalfe × Educational institutions / bootcamps (CS programs, product schools)

**Framework:** Network Effects + Reed/Metcalfe
**Segment:** Educational institutions / bootcamps (CS programs, product schools)
**Generated:** 2026-06-14

## The market direction in one sentence

CS departments and product schools license team-sdk as the shared substrate every cohort runs their capstone on, so cross-cohort loop libraries and claim corpora compound into the field's de facto teaching repository.

## What the framework reveals about this segment

Reed's Law says value scales as 2^N when participants form sub-groups, not just pairs. Education is one of the few segments where sub-group formation is structurally guaranteed: every cohort is a sub-group, every capstone team is a sub-group, every alumni cluster reforms in industry. If team-sdk becomes the substrate cohorts run on, the network compounds across three layers simultaneously — (a) Metcalfe-style pairwise value as cohorts swap loop patterns and `_signals.jsonl` retros, (b) Reed-style sub-group value as alumni form startup teams already fluent in the same Bus protocol, and (c) a knowledge-graph layer where every cohort's documented loops, decisions, and Halloway-ratcheted claims feed back into a public teaching corpus that next year's cohorts study. The dimension where adoption compounds is *pedagogical corpus*, not pricing.

## The team-sdk value proposition for this segment, via this framework

The configuration that lands hardest is a stripped Layer A (Constraints + Soul preserved verbatim, since these ARE the curriculum), an opinionated Layer B template per course archetype ("ship-a-feature", "incident-response", "discovery-to-MVP"), and aggressive emphasis on the Section 32 generative loops as graded artifacts. Students do not write essays about Critique-Revise; they run it and submit the loop trace. The four-cadence substrate maps cleanly onto course structure (loop = exercise, daily = standup, sprint = milestone, quarterly = capstone). The compounding wedge is the cross-institution claim corpus: every retro, every red-team, every decision a cohort logs becomes searchable teaching material for the next institution that adopts.

## GTM motion implied

Community-led, faculty-championed, with a foundation-style stewardship layer. Sales-led fails here (procurement cycles are 9-18 months, ARR ceiling is a few thousand per institution). The motion is to seed 3-5 lighthouse programs (one elite CS, one bootcamp, one product school, one international) with white-glove curriculum support, publish their cohort corpora as open teaching artifacts under a Creative Commons license, then let faculty peer-pressure do the rest at SIGCSE, Reinvent EDU, and bootcamp operator slack groups. Industry hiring partners co-fund the stewardship layer because every graduate ships with documented substrate fluency.

## Monetization shape

Hybrid: free institutional license + paid hosted corpus tier + revenue share on certified-graduate placements with industry hiring partners.

## Honest weaknesses of this alternative

The combination is genuinely awkward in three places. First, network effects in education compound on a 4-year clock, not a 4-quarter clock — by the 18-month winning condition deadline the loop has barely started. Second, the 50-teams-with-30%-friction-reduction success metric does not translate cleanly to cohorts that dissolve at semester end and never run a second quarter on the substrate. Third, students are not the teams the vision describes; they are practicing-teams, which means the "substrate compounds the team's judgment" promise is structurally weaker because the team itself does not persist. The strongest version of this alternative reframes the unit of compounding from "team" to "institution-as-meta-team", which strains the vision.
