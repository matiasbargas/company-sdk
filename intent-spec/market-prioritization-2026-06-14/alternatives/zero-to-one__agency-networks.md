## The market direction in one sentence

Practice leads at multi-client product consultancies adopt team-sdk as the substrate that turns every billable engagement into compounding network IP nobody else can replicate.

## What the framework reveals about this segment

The Thiel question is: what do you believe about agencies that almost nobody else believes? The conventional wisdom — and the reason every "agency knowledge management" play from McKinsey Knowledge to Deloitte's Athena has been mediocre — is that agencies are commodity services businesses where knowledge inevitably dies when projects close. The secret team-sdk encodes: a multi-tenant context substrate flips the agency P&L from "people-hours sold" to "judgment-corpus rented." Each client engagement deposits structured Layer B context (constraints, decisions, loops, retros) that not only accelerates *that* engagement's next phase but, with appropriate anonymization, contributes patterns to a network-level meta-corpus the agency owns and no individual client could ever build alone. The monopoly position is not "AI agents for agencies" — it is "the only substrate where doing client work makes you structurally smarter than every competitor next quarter." That is a 0-to-1 wedge because every incumbent assumes the knowledge-evaporation problem is unsolvable and prices accordingly.

## The team-sdk value proposition for this segment, via this framework

The configuration that lands: Layer A enforced at the network level (the agency's house principles, methodology, quality bars) with Layer B isolated per client tenant (their domains, constraints, decision history) and a controlled cross-tenant pattern layer where §32 generative loops mine recurring shapes (fintech onboarding patterns, marketplace cold-start patterns, B2B activation patterns) into reusable playbooks. The Halloway Ratchet doctrine (§24) becomes the agency's IP moat: every retro from every client tightens the network's shared judgment. Billable-time pressure makes the four-cadence metabolism immediately ROI-positive because partners can show clients the substrate compounding *their* knowledge inside *their* tenant, then quote a higher rate next engagement because onboarding cost drops by 60%.

## GTM motion implied

Partner-led, top-down, with a 0-to-1 twist: do not sell to 50 agencies — sell to one ambitious mid-tier network (60-300 people, 2-3 offices, partner-owned, hungry to escape the holding-company race-to-zero) and make them the reference monopoly in their tier. Their network advantage becomes visible to competitors within 18 months and the pull becomes inbound. This fits Thiel because monopoly requires concentration, not breadth, in the early years; one dominant reference is worth 50 logo customers.

## Monetization shape

Hybrid: per-tenant license floor (network-wide platform fee) plus value-based uplift tied to the agency's realized rate increase or utilization gain — agency keeps the upside, team-sdk takes a percentage of the structural margin lift.

## Honest weaknesses of this alternative

The combination is genuinely awkward in two places. First, the Thiel lens demands a defensible monopoly secret, and "multi-tenant agency substrate" is conceptually defensible but practically imitable — Palantir-style services moats took a decade to build and required deep customer entanglement team-sdk's lightweight philosophy resists. Second, agency networks are notoriously slow buyers, partner-owned, allergic to "platforms" that threaten their utilization model, and the decision-maker (a managing partner) is exactly the persona most likely to mistake team-sdk for "another knowledge management thing we tried in 2014." The 0-to-1 framing is right but the sales cycle may be 9-18 months per network, which is brutal for an early-stage substrate trying to hit 50 teams by December 2027.
