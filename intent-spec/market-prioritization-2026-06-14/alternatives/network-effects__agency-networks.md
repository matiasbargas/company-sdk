# Alternative: Network Effects + Reed/Metcalfe x Agency networks / product consultancies running multiple client teams

**Framework:** Network Effects + Reed/Metcalfe
**Segment:** Agency networks / product consultancies running multiple client teams
**Generated:** 2026-06-14

## The market direction in one sentence

Partners at digital product consultancies (10-200 billable staff) license team-sdk as the multi-tenant substrate every client pod runs on, so loop patterns and Layer B archetypes compound across the portfolio.

## What the framework reveals about this segment

A consultancy is already a network in disguise: N client engagements running in parallel, each pod re-inventing discovery rituals, retro formats, decision logs, and risk taxonomies because there is no substrate to carry pattern across the firewall. Metcalfe value here is per-firm (more pods on team-sdk inside Acme Studio means more cross-pod reuse of loop patterns and BU routings, value scales ~ N^2). Reed value is per-network (when 30 agencies share an anonymized corpus of loop patterns, gate-check archetypes, and "claims that survived red-team", the number of usable sub-group recombinations scales 2^N). The decisive insight: the segment is the *only* commercial buyer for whom the substrate getting smarter from peer adoption is a directly billable advantage — they sell judgment at hourly rates, and a substrate that compresses time-to-judgment is gross-margin expansion, not productivity theater.

## The team-sdk value proposition for this segment, via this framework

Ship team-sdk as a multi-tenant "Practice Substrate" where Layer A encodes the firm's craft DNA (Soul principles, methodology, gate criteria), Layer B per-client domain context stays sandboxed under NDA, and Layer C scratch is per-mission. The killer SKUs: (1) cross-pod *loop pattern library* — when one pod's Section 32 Critique-Revise pattern produces a high signal-to-action rate, it surfaces as a recommended pattern to peer pods next sprint; (2) firm-wide *gate-check archetypes* — CLO/CISO/Mario gates accumulate firm-specific precedent (e.g., "this is how our firm handles PII in fintech discovery"); (3) anonymized *cross-firm benchmarks* via opt-in federation — partners see how their pods' loop velocity and KR-movement attribution compare to peer firms in the same vertical. The four-cadence metabolism becomes the firm's quality system; Halloway Ratchet doctrine becomes their defensible methodology.

## GTM motion implied

Partner-led, then community-led. Sell first to one boutique consultancy (20-40 staff) where one partner-champion owns the methodology and can mandate substrate adoption across pods — value visible within one engagement cycle (8-12 weeks). Then run a quarterly "Practice Substrate Summit" that turns adopting firms into a community where loop patterns are traded — this is the Reed-value flywheel: firms come for the tool, stay for the peer corpus. Avoid enterprise sales-led motion until 5+ flagship firms anchor the network; the early network has to be hand-curated for craft-density, not revenue.

## Monetization shape

Hybrid: per-firm license (floor) + per-active-pod seat (scales with usage) + premium tier for cross-firm federated benchmarks.

## Honest weaknesses of this alternative

The combo is awkward in three places. First, consultancies are *culturally* allergic to standardized methodology — partners built their firms on bespoke judgment and will resist any substrate that smells like McKinsey-in-a-box; the "compounding judgment" pitch can read as "commoditizing our craft." Second, the Reed/Metcalfe value requires cross-firm federation that competes with the very NDA walls firms charge premium for — opt-in federation is real but the high-margin firms are the ones least likely to opt in, so the network may grow precisely among the firms whose patterns matter least. Third, billable-hour pressure cuts both ways: substrate ROI is immediate (true), but so is the partner's instinct to bill the substrate-build hours to clients, which converts team-sdk from infrastructure into a one-shot deliverable and kills compounding adoption. The framework predicts a flywheel; the segment's economics predict friction at the hub.
