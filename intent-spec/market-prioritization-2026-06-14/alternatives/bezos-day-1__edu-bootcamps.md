## The market direction in one sentence

CS programs and product schools license team-sdk as the curriculum substrate where student cohorts learn cross-functional product work by running real Bus loops, retros, and Section 32 critiques on capstone projects.

## What the framework reveals about this segment

Working Backwards forces us to draft the press release first. The headline that would matter on Day 1 of winning here reads: *"Graduates from the 2027 cohort arrive at their first job already fluent in cross-functional product loops — they have run 200+ Bus messages, shipped 6 retros, and survived a Mario gate before their first standup."* Working backwards from that, the FAQ surfaces the hard truth: educators do not buy "agent frameworks," they buy **graduate outcomes** and **employer signal**. The customer experience the press release demands is not a tool — it is a *learnable, gradable artifact corpus* that a TA can review, a hiring manager can scan, and an accreditation board can audit. That reframes team-sdk from "operating substrate" to "the portfolio format the industry recognizes." Day 1 thinking also reveals the long-term flywheel: today's students become tomorrow's adopters at their employers, so the bootcamp channel is the cheapest possible distribution for the 18-month winning condition.

## The team-sdk value proposition for this segment, via this framework

The configuration that lands hardest is a stripped Layer A (Constraints + Soul kept verbatim — these *are* the pedagogy), a Layer B pre-seeded with 4-6 canonical project domains (e-commerce, fintech, healthtech, marketplace), and a Section 32 Critique-Revise loop wired as the **grading rubric itself**. The four-cadence metabolism collapses to two: per-class-session loops and end-of-module retros, mapped to syllabus weeks. The Halloway Ratchet doctrine becomes the literacy outcome — students leave knowing how to make irreversible decisions reversibly. Most critically: the corpus a student graduates with is their portfolio. `_signals.jsonl` becomes a transcript.

## GTM motion implied

Partner-led, evolving to community-led. Day 1 working-backwards says: do not sell schools one-by-one. Land 3-5 lighthouse programs (one elite CS, one elite product school, one bootcamp, one community college) with co-designed curriculum and named faculty champions. The press release on Day 1 quotes the faculty champion, not us. From there, the alumni network carries adoption into their employers (the actual revenue segment), and the curriculum becomes an open standard other schools adopt to stay competitive on placement metrics.

## Monetization shape

Hybrid: low/free institutional license + per-seat student fee bundled into tuition + downstream employer-adoption attribution revenue share.

## Honest weaknesses of this alternative

Working Backwards is fundamentally a *customer obsession* framework, and the customer here is split three ways — faculty (buyer), students (user), employers (outcome consumer) — which is exactly the kind of fragmented stakeholder map Bezos warns against. The press release writes well but the FAQ collapses: who pays, who decides, and who churns are three different people on three different cycles. ARR is structurally low (segment notes confirm), so this can only justify itself as a distribution play for other segments — meaning it is not really a market direction, it is a marketing channel dressed as one. Also: academic procurement is glacial, which violates Day 1 velocity instincts.
