# CMO panel score — christensen-disruption__regulated-enterprise

**Reviewer:** CMO (M3)
**Date:** 2026-06-14
**Lens:** market context, positioning, competitive narrative, brand differentiation, launch viability

## Scores

- **Viability (CMO domain): 6/10** — The wedge is plausible but the positioning is in a fight with itself. The alternative wants to land as a developer/MLOps substrate AND emerge as an audit-grade artifact, and a single brand voice cannot serve both audiences without one defeating the other. Addressable through messaging discipline, but the alternative does not yet show it has been addressed. I would not sign off on a launch sequence until the dual-audience positioning is resolved into a single primary register.
- **Market growth potential (CMO domain): 8/10** — EU AI Act Article 12, DORA, FDA GMLP, NYDFS Part 500, and US state AI laws are creating real, time-bounded *market pull* for "make model and team decisions auditable without slowing the team down." ML platform leads are being pulled into compliance conversations they did not sign up for — that is the textbook moment for a category-defining positioning claim. Window is real but narrowing (18-24 months) before "AI governance substrate" gets commoditized through overuse.

## Risk not named (CMO-specific)

**The category-naming collision.** The ML platform lead and the CISO use different words for the same underlying problem. The ML lead says "experiment tracking / lineage / reproducibility / decision logs." The CISO says "model risk evidence / control attestation / SR 11-7 artifacts." A single homepage, a single positioning statement, and a single launch sequence cannot serve both audiences as primary — and the bottom-up motion the alternative requires means the **developer-side message must be primary brand**. The moment marketing publishes a site that leads with "audit-grade" or "compliance-ready," the ML platform lead — who arrived from a Slack link expecting a dev tool — bounces. But the moment marketing publishes a site that leads with "decision substrate for ML pods," the CISO who eventually finds it cannot recognize it as the thing they need to budget for. The alternative treats this as a sales-cycle problem (procurement gauntlet, vendor risk review). It is actually a **launch-sequence and brand-architecture problem** that has to be solved BEFORE the wedge ships — likely via two-layer site architecture (primary dev surface, deep "compliance officers, start here" path) and disciplined refusal to use compliance language in any acquisition channel. The alternative does not name this and it is the single most likely cause of stalled adoption.

## Upside undersold (CMO-specific)

**The evidence-export pack is the distribution mechanism, not a feature.** The alternative frames evidence-export as a monetization wedge ("paid per-team evidence-export and framework-mapping packs"). The bigger marketing reality: in regulated enterprises, when a Big-4 audit team or an internal audit function encounters an evidence pack that is *visibly* better organized than what they normally receive — provenance threaded, decisions traceable to people and dates, §32 red-team challenges logged with resolutions, Halloway Ratchet doctrinal entries showing why an irreversible call was made — that artifact becomes its own marketing artifact. Audit teams talk to other audit teams. Compliance officers compare notes in industry working groups (FFIEC councils, EU AI Act working groups, HIMSS, model-risk meetups). One well-shaped evidence pack from a top-15 BFSI or top-20 health-payer ML team becomes the case study that opens the next ten doors *without any paid acquisition spend*. This is the regulated-enterprise equivalent of "the product is the marketing" loop the devtool segments get for free — but the alternative did not claim it. Properly framed, the evidence pack functions as a **lateral viral artifact across an industry where lateral trust is the only trust that travels**, and the launch sequence should be designed around producing two or three flagship packs in the first 12 months as the primary brand investment, not as a side effect of customer success.

## Verdict: **probe**

The market timing is right and the upside (evidence pack as viral artifact) is real, but the dual-audience positioning problem is unresolved and the messaging discipline required to thread the needle is exacting and unforgiving. A go would commit a launch sequence we do not yet have a credible positioning statement for. A kill would discard a segment whose wave is timed correctly for our 18-month winning condition. Probe means: spin one design-partner motion with disciplined dev-first positioning, hold launch copy until we can pass the "so what?" test for the ML platform lead in one sentence without using a compliance word, and measure whether the evidence-export artifact actually creates the lateral pull I expect.

Specifically I would probe:
1. Does an ML platform lead, shown the homepage cold, describe the product to a peer in one sentence — and does that sentence contain zero compliance language?
2. Does an internal audit lead, shown a sample evidence pack from a design partner, ask "can I show this to my counterpart at [peer institution]?" within 30 minutes? If yes, the viral artifact thesis holds and this segment graduates from probe to go.
3. Within 90 days, does the design partner pod's `_signals.jsonl` show the 30% friction-reduction signature in *developer* workflows (review cycles, decision recall, post-mortem authoring)? Compliance-evidence is the byproduct; if the developer signal is absent, the wedge is a compliance-tool pitch in disguise and the alternative collapses to a kill.
