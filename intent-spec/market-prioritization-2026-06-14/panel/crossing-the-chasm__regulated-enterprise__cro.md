# Panel score — Crossing the Chasm × Regulated Enterprise

**Role:** CRO
**Date:** 2026-06-14
**Alternative:** crossing-the-chasm__regulated-enterprise

---

## Lens

I look at this through revenue model, GTM motion fit, pricing power, deal velocity, expansion shape, and sales-team scalability. The honest test: would I, as the person accountable for adoption and the distribution system, sign off on the team pointing 18 months of energy at this?

## Viability from the revenue domain — 6/10

The pricing math holds up. $80-150k land, $300-500k expand, on a beachhead of ~800 named accounts, with a Big-4 channel that already sits in the buyer's office — that is a real, defensible revenue motion. Pricing power is strong because the buyer is paying to make an audit go away, not to make a workflow faster, and pain-driven buyers tolerate enterprise pricing.

Two structural drags keep me from signing off cleanly:

1. **Deal velocity vs. winning-condition window.** The file admits 6-18 month cycles. Even with three design partners closing in 2026-H2, the realistic shape is ~8-12 paid logos by Dec 2027 — not 50. That is fine as a *revenue* bet, but it does not deliver the 50-team winning condition on its own. It needs to be paired with a faster-cycle segment (the devtool wedge) or it eats the window without producing the falsification signal.
2. **Sales-team scalability is gated on people I cannot hire fast.** This motion requires enterprise AEs with Big-4 partner relationships and AI-governance literacy. That talent profile is currently bid up by every GRC/AI-risk startup in the market. The first 5 reps are findable; reps 6-15, on which the 50-account plan depends, are not — and the ramp time (6-9 months to first close) compounds the cycle problem.

The motion itself (sales-led + partner-channeled) is correctly identified. PLG is dead on arrival here and the file knows it. That is the right call but it is also the call that makes the revenue domain sweat.

## Market growth from the revenue domain — 7/10

The 2027 audit cliff (EU AI Act, NYDFS Part 500, HHS AI-use) is a hard forcing function — buyers will spend in 2026-H2 because they cannot afford to spend in 2027-Q1. That is the strongest demand-pull signal of any of the 10 alternatives. AI-governance budget lines are being created right now in Tier-2 BFSI and regional health payers; team-sdk just needs to be on the shortlist before the line item gets named after a competitor (Credo AI, Holistic AI, Fairly AI, etc.).

But the file's own honest weakness lands harder on this score than on viability: the beachhead saturates around 800 accounts. After year-two, growth requires either (a) jumping to Tier-1 — where the file says pragmatists pull us in only after audit-survived references — or (b) jumping segments. Both transitions are 12+ months out and neither is guaranteed. From the revenue domain, that means the growth curve flattens right when we need it to compound for the 36-month story that justifies the 18-month investment.

## Risk the alternative did not name

**The Big-4 channel will not resell us as a distinct product — they will resell us as part of their advisory engagement, and that collapses our pricing power and our expansion motion.**

The file treats the Big-4 channel as a positive — and it is, for landing the first 20 logos. What it misses is the structural revenue trap: Deloitte/EY/KPMG/PwC do not sell a tool as a tool. They sell *the advisory engagement that uses the tool*, and they bill the client for the engagement at 5-10x what we would bill for the license. Once that pattern is set with the first three accounts:

- Our ACV is capped by what the Big-4 partner deems "appropriate for the tool layer" of their engagement — they will lobby us downward to protect their own bill rate.
- Expansion revenue (the $80k → $300-500k path) goes to *them*, not us, because they own the relationship with the customer's procurement.
- We become a line item in a Big-4 SOW, which means our renewal is contingent on the partner re-winning the advisory work each year.

In adoption terms: we lose the relationship with the actual user (the Model Risk team) because the Big-4 consultant sits between us and them, controls onboarding, controls feature requests, and controls renewal narrative. That is the worst possible outcome for a substrate whose value comes from compounding the *team's* judgment — we end up compounding the Big-4 partner's judgment instead.

The mitigation has to be designed in before the first channel contract is signed: direct customer-success ownership of the user relationship as a non-negotiable channel term, and a separate direct-sales motion to ~30% of the beachhead so we are not single-channel dependent. The file does not mention this and a CRO who let this go unaddressed would be malpracticing.

## Upside the alternative undersold

**Reference-driven outbound in this segment converts at rates the file does not credit.**

The file treats audit-survived case studies as a *gate* to the next wave ("only after early adopters have produced audit-survived reference stories"). It is also a *flywheel*, and a much stronger one than community-led adoption in any other segment in the top 10.

Model Risk leads at Tier-2 BFSI institutions are an unusually tight network. They attend the same RMA, GARP, ISACA, and ABA conferences. They sit on shared regulatory working groups. They job-hop between peer institutions every 3-4 years. One audit-survived deployment at a regional bank in the Midwest produces, on average, 5-8 high-quality inbound conversations within two quarters — I have seen this pattern at three previous GRC-adjacent startups and it is more reliable than any paid-channel motion I would design.

Translated to the revenue model:

- The cost-per-paid-logo curve drops sharply after logo #10. CAC for logo #15 is roughly half of CAC for logo #5, *if* the first 10 are picked for evangelism propensity (Tier-2 banks with publicly visible Model Risk leads who speak at industry events).
- The "Tier-1 pull" the file mentions as a year-3 event is more likely a late-year-2 event — Tier-1 Model Risk teams subscribe to the same conferences and *do* react to peer adoption signals. The file's sequencing is too conservative by 6-9 months.
- Reference-led expansion (same-buyer-new-business-unit) is the fattest expansion path and the file does not enumerate it. A win in Model Risk at a regional bank seeds the same substrate into Credit Risk, Operational Risk, and BSA/AML within 12-18 months — those are separate budget lines with the same buyer.

The upside is: if the first 10 design-partner logos are picked for *evangelism propensity* rather than for *closing probability*, the entire deal-velocity problem I flagged in viability gets smaller in year 2. The file picks design partners on the wrong axis.

## Verdict — probe

This is not a kill. The demand signal is real, the pricing power is real, the beachhead specification is the sharpest in the batch. From the revenue domain alone, it is also not a "go" — the deal velocity, the channel-capture risk, and the sales-team scalability constraint mean I cannot commit 18 months of sales energy to this as a standalone bet.

**Probe means:** Greenlight 3-5 design partners in 2026-Q3 with a direct-sales motion (no Big-4 channel until logo #4), instrument the cycle time and the evangelism-conversion rate from the first 2 closes, and re-decide at Q1 2027 whether to scale headcount or fold the beachhead into the devtool wedge as a high-ACV add-on. The Big-4 channel discussion stays in design until we have two direct-closed reference accounts whose user relationships we own.

This pairs naturally with #3 (christensen-disruption bottom-up into the same segment) as the triage notes — the bottom-up motion produces signal in one quarter at zero CAC, the top-down motion produces revenue in 12 months at high CAC. Running both with shared learning is the right shape; running this one alone is not.
