# CRO deep-score — porter-5-forces × agency-networks

**Role:** CRO (Adwoa Accra — adoption-as-system lens)
**Alternative:** `porter-5-forces__agency-networks`
**Date:** 2026-06-14

## Lens applied

Revenue model, GTM motion fit, pricing power, deal velocity, expansion shape, sales-team scalability — read against the 18-month winning condition (50 paying teams, ≥30% friction reduction QoQ for 2 consecutive quarters).

## What the alternative gets right (revenue lens)

- **Per-engagement license unit is the right shape.** "Active client engagement" is a natural billing primitive — it scales with the buyer's revenue, not with our seat count, and it survives team churn inside the agency. This is the strongest pricing-power signal in the file.
- **Partner-controlled budget collapses procurement to one signature.** Compared to the devtool wedge (no procurement at all) and regulated (6-18 month vendor risk reviews), agency partners can write a $20-80k pilot check inside one conversation. Deal velocity is the under-appreciated asset here.
- **Multi-tenant Layer B is the natural expansion engine.** Each new client engagement is a new revenue unit attached to an existing logo — pure NRR mechanics. CAC is paid once per agency; LRV compounds with their client roster.
- **Counter-positioning against new entrants is a real wedge that the buyer FEELS.** Selling to a buyer with acute structural pain compresses the discovery-to-close cycle dramatically.

## Where the alternative is weaker than it claims (revenue lens)

- The self-score of V8 assumes the GTM motion runs at partner velocity. But partner-led sales at agencies have a specific failure mode the file doesn't name (see Risk Not Named below) — it's not the buyer's pain that gates the close, it's the **billable-hour accounting model** that has to bend around the substrate. The license-vs-utilization conflict is unresolved.
- "Channel partnerships with Bureau of Digital / SoDA" is a paper-thin distribution claim. Those associations are coordination bodies, not distribution channels. They produce one conference and a Slack — they do not produce a pipeline.
- The 90-day pilot framing assumes the agency can route real client engagement signal into `_signals.jsonl` inside 90 days. Most agency engagements are 4-12 weeks. Signal continuity across short engagements is structurally weaker than at devtools (continuous teams) or regulated (continuous compliance programs). This compresses the time window in which the substrate can prove its 30% friction-reduction signature inside any single agency.

## Scoring

### Viability (CRO sign-off, 0-10): **6**

I would conditionally sign off, with two non-negotiable wedge requirements:

1. **A pricing structure that doesn't collide with billable-hour accounting** (sketched below in Upside).
2. **A maintainer-led first-5-agencies motion** before any "top-100 outreach" plan. The file jumps to top-100 outreach as if it were a known motion. It is not. We have run zero agency conversations. The first 5 agencies are CRO-direct, founder-led, high-touch — and only after those 5 do we know what the repeatable motion is. A V8 score requires evidence that does not yet exist; V6 is honest.

### Market growth potential (CRO domain, 0-10): **5**

- Bounded TAM (low thousands of agencies globally, per the file — and realistically the addressable subset is 400-800 product consultancies above 15 people that have product-team substrate at all).
- The segment is contracting under AI pressure (the very dynamic the alternative leverages). Consolidation may shrink the buyer pool faster than we acquire it.
- ACV is high per logo but logo count is finite — this is a "big deals, few of them" segment, which is structurally a CRO concern when the winning condition is **50 logos**. If agency = 1 logo each (with multi-engagement expansion inside), we'd need to acquire 50 agencies, which is roughly 10% of the realistic addressable pool. That's an aggressive penetration target for an 18-month window with no prior agency motion in place.
- The expansion shape is genuinely good (per-engagement NRR), but expansion does not count toward the 50-logo milestone — it counts toward the *second* 18 months.

## Risk Not Named (CRO domain)

**The billable-hour accounting collision is the deal-killer the file does not name.** Agency P&L is constructed around utilization rate × bill rate × hours. team-sdk asks senior consultants to invest unbillable hours building the Layer A craft constraints and Layer B per-client corpus — work that creates compounding firm value but **destroys this-quarter utilization metrics**. The partner who signed the contract has to sell the substrate internally against the practice's utilization-driven bonus pool. Every senior consultant whose Q3 bonus depends on 85% utilization will quietly route around the substrate, because contributing to it doesn't bill.

This is not a positioning problem — it is a revenue-system collision that kills agency tools repeatedly (see: every PSA tool that promised "knowledge management" since 2010). The substrate either has to (a) make the contribution itself billable as a contracted client deliverable (which forces a specific contract shape we have not designed), or (b) ship a partner-level dashboard that shows the compounding firm-value delta the contribution produces, so partners can override the utilization metric with a defensible alternative. Neither is in the file. Both are gates on the V8 self-score.

A related sub-risk the CRO desk sees: **agency CRMs are political objects**. Salesforce-of-record is the partner's territory; ops-of-record is the COO's territory; PSA (utilization) is the CFO's territory. The substrate touches all three and owns none. Whichever partner championed it leaves the agency or rotates roles inside 24 months, and adoption collapses. The motion needs a co-champion structure (partner + practice ops lead) from the first conversation, not as a remediation later.

## Upside Undersold (CRO domain)

**The end-of-engagement Layer B export is a revenue mechanism the file frames as a Constraint 1 mitigation — but it is actually a top-of-funnel growth flywheel the file does not value.**

The JTBD pair-alternative (#7) gestures at "exportable to client at end-of-engagement" as a Constraint 1 fix. From a CRO lens, the export is much more than that — it is the **client-side acquisition channel that runs without our sales team**. Every engagement that ships a Layer B corpus to a client puts team-sdk artifacts inside that client's team. If even 1 in 10 of those clients adopts team-sdk directly within 6 months (a realistic conversion rate for a substrate already proving daily value against their open issues), then each agency on team-sdk becomes a distributed sales force generating qualified inbound at zero marginal CAC.

Math sketch: 30 agencies × 8 active engagements each × 10% export-to-adoption = 24 new direct-team logos per quarter from agency-channel alone, after one steady-state quarter. That is half the entire 18-month winning condition arriving as **inbound from the agency channel acting as a distribution layer**, not as direct sales. The file scores this segment as "non-correlated diversified revenue" — that is structurally wrong. It is the **single highest-leverage distribution channel in the entire top 10** if the export mechanic is engineered with this growth loop as a first-class goal, not as a Constraint 1 afterthought.

This reframes the entire ROI of the agency motion. The 6 score on market growth ignores the indirect channel value. The growth potential is 8 if we treat agencies as distribution + revenue, not as revenue only.

## Verdict: **probe**

The economics are real and the structural pain in the buyer is real, but four named gates have to clear before this becomes a "go" for the CRO desk:

1. A first-5-agencies founder-led wedge that proves the motion is reproducible at all (kill criterion: if conversations 1-5 don't yield 2 paid pilots, the motion is wrong, not the substrate).
2. A pricing structure designed against billable-hour accounting collision — explicit contract shape that makes substrate contribution billable or otherwise non-cannibalizing of utilization.
3. The end-of-engagement export mechanic engineered as a growth loop with attribution back to the source agency (revenue-share or referral-credit possibly), not as a generic feature.
4. A co-champion (partner + practice ops) onboarding requirement before any pilot starts, so the motion survives the partner's role rotation.

Pair this probe with the JTBD agency alternative (#7) — same segment, opposite framing, can share the same first-5-agency design partners. Run both probes in parallel under the same partner relationships, decide after 90 days which framing converts faster.

Do not invest in top-100 outreach, Bureau of Digital channel work, or any scaled motion until the four gates clear. Premature scaling of an agency motion is the most expensive way to learn the motion is wrong.

## Summary line for the panel

**probe** — V6/G5 in isolation, V7/G8 if the end-of-engagement export is reframed as the primary growth loop and the billable-hour collision is solved at the contract layer. The upside is bigger than the alternative author saw; the operational risk is real and unowned.
