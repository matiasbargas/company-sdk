# CEO panel score — jobs-to-be-done__agency-networks

**Scored by:** Greg (CEO)

## My vantage on this alternative

The CEO lens is: does this direction sharpen what we are, narrow what we will NOT build, and produce an 18-month win that compounds rather than fragments? The JTBD frame here is unusually clean — "make a new pod sound institutionally competent on day three, not week three" is the kind of one-sentence job that the whole company can rally around. That is rare and valuable. Most of the 50 alternatives produced segment+vehicle combinations; this one produced a *job statement* that would survive a partner meeting unmodified.

## Viability from CEO vantage (0-10): 6

I would sign off on the team executing this **as a paired bet with Porter agency (#6), not standalone**. Three CEO-level reasons:

1. **It is genuinely Constraint-1-friendly.** The "Layer B exports to the client at engagement end" twist is the single cleanest answer in the entire 50-alternative batch to the Owner's anti-lock-in principle. Most agency plays drift toward methodology vault; this one structurally cannot, because the deliverable IS the corpus. That is a real strategic asset, not a marketing line.

2. **The job is universal across the segment but the buyer signal is small.** Agency partners do hire for this job — every partner I have ever sat with has the "is this pod ready for the CFO?" anxiety. But the segment is structurally bad at buying internal infra (the file admits this). The viability question is not "is the job real" — it is "will partners convert inside 18 months at the pace we need?" I do not believe a single-frame JTBD pitch closes 15-20 agencies in that window without the Porter structural-pressure framing riding alongside.

3. **The "what we are NOT building" discipline this would impose is healthy.** Choosing agency-JTBD as a top bet forces us to explicitly NOT build: knowledge-management positioning, individual-contributor productivity framing, simulated-team scenarios, certification programs. Those exclusions are good for the company regardless of whether this bet wins.

I score 6 because the job is excellent but the segment's procurement metabolism is not. A 7-8 would require either (a) a paired structural-pressure narrative (Porter) running on the same accounts, or (b) a wedge product that lands inside one billable cycle. As a solo bet, I do not see how it hits 50 teams in 18 months.

## Market growth from CEO vantage (0-10): 4

The CEO lens on market growth is not "is the market big" — it is "is the dynamic moving toward us or away from us across the 18-month window?" The triage scored this 5; I score it 4, lower than triage, for a CEO-specific reason:

The agency category is consolidating under AI-native competitor pressure. The triage report correctly notes this cuts both ways — consolidators need this more. But from where I sit, the *speed* of consolidation matters more than the *direction*. If 30% of mid-tier agencies in our target segment are acquired, merged, or restructured during our 18-month window, our pipeline conversion math gets shredded mid-execution. That is not a market-size problem; it is a market-stability problem, and CEOs are the role responsible for noticing when the ground is moving under a multi-quarter bet. The TAM is fine. The pipeline volatility across the window is the issue.

I would revise this upward to 6 if the file had named one concrete signal that agency consolidation accelerates demand for our substrate rather than disrupting our buying motion. It did not.

## Risk the alternative did not name (CEO-specific)

**Strategic identity dilution: if we win on this bet, we become "the agency substrate company" in the market's perception, and that brand gravity actively repels the devtool wedge (#1, #4, #5, #8, #9) which is our primary metabolism-proof zone.** The file scoped its risks to multi-tenancy engineering and agency consolidation — both real. What it did not name is the **positioning interference between this bet and the devtool wedge**. Dev-tool CTOs do not buy from "the substrate that helps consultancies look smart to clients." Agency partners do not buy from "the observability layer for product cognition." These two narrative lines are not just different — they are mutually erosive at the brand level. A company can run both as parallel GTM motions ONLY if it has a parent narrative that subsumes both without privileging either. Right now we do not have one. The risk this alternative did not name is that picking it without a unifying narrative pre-commits the company to a strategic identity that quietly kills our highest-metabolism bet.

This is a CEO risk because no domain agent owns brand-gravity-interference between two simultaneously-pursued segments. Triage saw the segments as complementary on revenue (devtool=metabolism, agency=diversification); from the brand level they are NOT obviously complementary, and treating them as such is a mistake only the CEO is positioned to flag.

## Upside the alternative undersold (CEO-specific)

**This is the bet that most cleanly produces a written articulation of the "Layer A vs Layer B" boundary that team-sdk has been struggling to communicate.** The file framed the per-engagement Layer B as a Constraint-1 mitigation (which it is). What it undersold is that **the agency-engagement structure FORCES the cleanest possible separation between substrate-as-our-IP (Layer A) and corpus-as-client-property (Layer B)** — because the legal MSA framework REQUIRES it. Every other segment lets us be sloppy about this boundary. The agency segment cannot.

Why this matters at the CEO level: the Layer A / Layer B distinction is the single hardest thing to explain about team-sdk to a new prospect, a new investor, or a new hire. If the agency bet forces us to ship that articulation in production-grade legal language inside one engagement, we get the most valuable strategic communication artifact in our entire roadmap — one that then makes every devtool and regulated pitch sharper for free. The agency bet's strategic export to the rest of the company is bigger than its direct revenue. The file scored this as "diversification" — that undersells it. It is also a **clarity-forcing function** for the company's core narrative, which is worth a quarter of CEO time even if the bet itself converts slowly.

## Verdict: probe

I will not vote **go**: as a standalone bet it does not hit the winning condition in time, and the brand-interference risk with the devtool wedge is unmitigated.

I will not vote **kill**: the JTBD framing is the cleanest in the batch, the Constraint-1-aligned deliverable model is genuinely innovative, and the clarity-forcing function on Layer A/B articulation is uniquely valuable.

**Probe** with two specific conditions:
1. **One design partner, not three.** A single mid-tier consultancy, instrumented hard, used to falsify or confirm the "day-three fluency" delta inside one billable cycle (8-12 weeks). The probe succeeds if the partner WhatsApp network produces inbound from peer firms inside one quarter. It fails if the partner loves it but no peer firm self-identifies.
2. **Co-run with Porter agency (#6) on the same account.** Test whether the structural-pressure narrative (#6) and the job-shaped narrative (#7) compound in the same sales motion or compete for partner attention. If they compound: graduate both to **go**. If they compete: pick one based on which one closed.

Probe budget: one engineer-quarter on multi-tenancy isolation, one design-partner contract at deep discount, one Greg-personally-runs-the-relationship commitment. Decision point: end of Q4 2026, based on inbound velocity from the partner network and the Porter/JTBD compounding test.

What this probe MUST NOT do: pre-commit the company's brand to "the agency substrate company" until the brand-gravity question is resolved.
