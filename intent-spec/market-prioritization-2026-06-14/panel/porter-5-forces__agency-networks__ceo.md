# Panel score — Porter's Five Forces × Agency networks (CEO)

**Scorer:** Greg (CEO)
**Date:** 2026-06-14
**Role lens:** strategic clarity, 18-month winning condition, what NOT to build, the bet that shapes the company.

## Strategic framing

This is the diversification bet I selected at #6 in triage. Re-scoring it from the CEO lens — not the triage lens — means asking a different question: not "does it deserve a top-10 slot," but "would I sign off on the team executing this direction over the next 18 months?"

The 18-month winning condition is 50 paying teams with `_signals.jsonl` showing ≥30% friction reduction Q-over-Q for two consecutive quarters. The CEO question is whether *agency-network teams* can produce that signal — not whether agency partners will sign contracts.

## Viability (CEO lens): 6 / 10

The structural pain is real and the buyer (partner) controls budget — those are necessary conditions and they are present. But three things hold the score under the alternative's self-score of 8:

1. **Constraint 1 tension is unresolved.** The "methodology vault" frame leans hard toward lock-in; the alternative names this and then does not resolve it. Selling to agencies pulls the product narrative toward "your craft, codified and defensible" — which is not the substrate-compounds-judgment story the company is supposed to tell.
2. **Attention competes with the proof zone.** The devtool wedge is where the winning condition gets falsified. Agency multi-tenancy is unbuilt engineering. Every week spent on agency-side enablement is a week not spent sharpening the segment that decides whether the thesis is true at all.
3. **Partner-led sale is slow.** Even at 3-6 month cycles, design partners signed in Q3 2026 produce two engagement-cycles of signal by Q3 2027 — barely inside the window.

I sign off on a probe, not a parallel GTM motion.

## Market growth potential (CEO lens): 5 / 10

The author's self-score is 6. I dock one point for a dynamic the file undersells: **the buyer population is contracting during our winning window.** Porter's lens correctly diagnoses that agency networks are under structural pressure from AI-native entrants and foundation-model commoditization of senior judgment. The agencies that need us most are the ones losing accounts fastest. We are selling life rafts to a fleet that is shrinking. That is not a growth market in the CEO's sense of the word — it is a survival market, where some buyers convert urgently and some go under before procurement closes. Steady-state revenue, headwind-shaped TAM.

## Risk not named (the one only the CEO lens spots)

**Principal-agent signal corruption.** The alternative names methodology-vault soul tension and partner political resistance to Layer A standardization. It misses that the *buyer* (partner) and the *user* (consultant on engagement) have systematically misaligned incentives, and this corrupts `_signals.jsonl` — our north-star metric — in exactly the segment where we'd most want clean signal to validate the cross-engagement compounding thesis.

- **Partner incentive:** make `_signals.jsonl` show compounding methodology so it functions as a sales asset for the next pitch.
- **Consultant incentive:** finish billable engagements without documenting friction that reflects badly on the team.

The friction surfaced in the corpus ends up shaped by what makes the firm look good to the next client, not by what's actually causing rework. The devtool segment does not have this problem — engineers document friction because removing it makes their next PR easier; buyer and user are the same person. Agencies break that alignment by construction.

This matters because if we accept agency-segment signal as evidence of the winning condition, and the signal is incentive-distorted, we will claim a victory we have not actually earned. That is a worse outcome than missing the target — it builds the wrong thing confidently for two years.

## Upside undersold (the one only the CEO lens spots)

The alternative scopes the value at agency P&L: "partners control budget, ACV is high, multi-tenant is native." All true. But it misses the *narrative-distribution* value, which from the CEO's chair is larger than the revenue.

**Agency partners are the highest-bandwidth credibility transmitters into the segments we actually want to land in.** They write thought-leadership, sit on conference panels, advise Fortune-500 product orgs, and — crucially — talk to other partners and to the in-house product leaders at every client they serve. One Thoughtworks-tier reference doesn't just close three more agencies; it gives us the case-study language and the proof point to sell to the in-house product team at every client of theirs.

The agency bet, sized correctly (1-2 high-signal design partners, not a parallel sales motion), is a Trojan horse that lands the substrate-as-judgment-asset story inside the product orgs that are the real long-game buyer population. That is a non-correlation benefit the file mentions; what it does not name is that the *information flow* of that diversification is asymmetric in our favor — they pay us in dollars, we extract narrative ammunition for every other segment.

## Verdict: PROBE

Not go: signal-corruption risk and Constraint 1 tension are both unresolved, and the segment competes for attention against the proof zone.
Not kill: the narrative-distribution upside and the non-correlation are real, and the structural pain is sharp enough that one or two agencies will commit fast.

**Probe shape:** 1-2 design partners maximum, selected for *narrative reach* (Thoughtworks-tier or close), not for ACV. Contract terms must require us to publish a case study. Build the minimum multi-tenancy needed for two clients per design partner — no more. Measure success on whether the agency-segment `_signals.jsonl` reproduces the friction-delta signature the devtool wedge produces, *and* whether the case study converts at least one in-house product org. If both, expand. If neither, kill in Q1 2027.

## What this bet should NOT include

- No parallel sales motion to the top-100 consultancies. That is what the alternative implies; I refuse it at this stage.
- No general multi-tenancy engineering investment beyond what the 1-2 design partners require.
- No positioning of the substrate as a "methodology vault" or "moat." Constraint 1 is non-negotiable; if the narrative slips that direction, we lose more than we gain.
- No channel partnership with Bureau of Digital / SoDA in 2026. Premature; would commit to a GTM motion the probe hasn't validated.
