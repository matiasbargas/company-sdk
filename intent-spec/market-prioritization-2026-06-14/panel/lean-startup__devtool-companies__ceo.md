# CEO panel scoring — lean-startup__devtool-companies

**Scored by:** Greg (CEO)
**Date:** 2026-06-14
**Lens:** strategic clarity, 18-month winning condition, what to NOT build, the bet that shapes the company.

## Viability from CEO lens: 8/10

I would sign off on the team executing this direction. The reason is narrow and specific: the winning condition in `_product-vision.md` is *measured on `_signals.jsonl`*, not on revenue. This alternative is the only one in the top 10 whose primary unit of work — a 90-day MVL on one dev-tooling pod — is structurally identical to the falsification test for our own thesis. It is not a market bet; it is the experiment that tells us whether the thesis is worth any market bet at all. Strategic clarity does not get cleaner than that.

The point taken off the score is honest: the GTM motion (community-led, dogfood-first, lighthouse-published) caps revenue capture for an OSS-and-design-partner shape, and 50 *paying* teams by Dec 2027 is the winning condition — not 50 forks. The alternative admits this and proposes hybrid monetization without resolving how seat-scale revenue ever lands. I can sign off on the experiment without signing off on the monetization model; those are separate calls.

## Market growth potential from CEO lens: 6/10

The dev-tooling segment is finite (~hundreds globally per the alternative; consistent with what I see). From the CEO lens, growth potential is bounded by two facts the alternative acknowledges: (1) NIH norms push toward fork-and-self-host rather than buy; (2) each lighthouse is a credibility multiplier into adjacent segments but does not, by itself, expand TAM inside dev-tooling. The growth case is *not* "this segment scales to 50 paying teams" — it is "this segment proves the thesis cheaply, then the regulated and agency bets carry the revenue weight." That is a strong strategic position but a moderate market-growth position. A 6 reflects: I do not expect this segment to be the place we book most of the 50 by Dec 2027; I expect it to be the place we *earn the right to sell* to the segments that do.

## Risk the alternative did not name

**Lighthouse-dependency cascade risk.** The alternative names NIH, Lean Startup retread, and monetization caps. What it does not name — and only the CEO lens spots, because it is a company-shape risk rather than a domain risk — is that when you pick 3-5 lighthouses and let their `_signals.jsonl` be a *published artifact*, your narrative becomes hostage to their execution. If a lighthouse has a bad quarter for reasons unrelated to team-sdk (layoffs, founder drama, a pivot, a bungled launch), the public corpus shows friction *not* decreasing — and the dev-tools ecosystem reads that as team-sdk failing, not the lighthouse failing. There is no contractual or strategic mechanism that protects us from this. We are concentrating reputational risk on a denominator of three to five companies we do not control. The mitigation is sequencing (one lighthouse first, prove the curve, then add the next two only after the public signal is unambiguous), but the alternative jumps to "3-5 lighthouses" without a sequencing constraint. That constraint is non-negotiable for me.

## Upside the alternative undersold

**The recruiting and credibility flywheel into the team that builds team-sdk itself.** The alternative frames this segment as "strategic insurance" — proves the thesis on the hardest crowd. What it undersells, and what only the CEO lens sees clearly because it is about company shape rather than market dynamics, is that a dev-tooling company publicly running on team-sdk is *simultaneously* a recruiting magnet for the engineers we need to hire to build team-sdk's next phase, *and* a sales reference for every other segment we touch. The regulated buyer who hears "the team that ships [observability tool you use] runs their roadmap on this substrate, in public" has been pre-sold on technical credibility before our first call. The agency partner who sees a published lighthouse `_signals.jsonl` has a working artifact to show their own clients. The substrate's value to *us* — as a hiring and credibility instrument across all three bets — may exceed its value to the lighthouse customer inside the 18-month window. The alternative scores this as a 7 on viability and 6 on growth; both numbers under-count the compounding effect on the *other* bets in the portfolio.

## Verdict: GO — with a sequencing non-negotiable

This is not a probe. The winning condition is measured on `_signals.jsonl`, and this alternative is the only one whose primary unit of work *is* a `_signals.jsonl` curve over 90 days on a credibility-multiplier segment. Killing it leaves the regulated bet without a proof point and the agency bet without a credibility anchor.

**Non-negotiable I am attaching to the GO:** one lighthouse first. Not three. Not five. One dev-tooling pod, 90 days, public `_signals.jsonl`, and we do not add the second lighthouse until the first one's friction-delta curve is visible to the dev-tools ecosystem. This sequencing is what differentiates a GO from a probe: we commit to the experiment, we do not commit to the scaled lighthouse motion until the experiment lands.

**What we are NOT building, in this bet:** we are not building a paid PLG funnel for dev-tooling companies inside the 18-month window. The monetization for this segment is OSS + design-partner contracts during lighthouse phase, full stop. Anyone on the team who proposes a seat-scale pricing experiment in this segment before December 2027 is optimizing for the wrong thing — that is what the regulated triad is for.

## How this fits the portfolio (per triage cluster note)

The triage cluster note correctly identifies this as one of five devtool-wedge alternatives (#1, #4, #5, #8, #9) running in parallel against the same target population. I read this as: #1 (Christensen, anchor) supplies the *market-position* framing, #4 (Zero-to-One, narrative) supplies the *messaging*, #5 (Lean Startup, this one) supplies the *measurement instrument*, #8 (Blue Ocean) supplies the *category claim*, #9 (Crossing-the-Chasm) supplies the *sequencing discipline*. Five lenses, one segment, one experiment. This alternative is the measurement spine the other four are written against. Without it, the devtool wedge is rhetoric without falsification.
