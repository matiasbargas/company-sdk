## Scores

- **Viability (CEO sign-off):** 7
- **Market growth potential (strategic dynamism):** 5

### Viability reasoning

I would sign off on the team executing in this direction **conditionally** — not as the anchor bet, but as the sequencing layer beneath the broader devtool wedge (#1, #4, #5, #8). Crossing-the-Chasm's actual contribution here is *discipline about which 8-12 logos go first and in what order the bowling pins fall* — not a fresh market thesis. The alt itself is honest that it's awkward (NIH risk, the beachhead may not transfer to non-devtool pragmatists). Where it shines from my chair: it forces us to name the 8-12 specific Series-B platform-eng vendors and refuse to chase the other 188. That's exactly the "what we are NOT building, and why is that decision as important as what we are building" question I owe the team in writing.

What discounts it to a 7 rather than higher: the alt is the *sequencing layer* of a bet, not the bet itself. As a standalone direction it would be hollow — Chasm without a deeper category claim (Blue Ocean #8) or narrative engine (Zero-to-One #4) underneath gives us a procurement playbook without product-marketing soul. I sign off on this *as a sub-tactic of the devtool wedge*, not as a parallel strategic stream.

### Market growth potential reasoning

Score of 5 reflects strategic honesty, not pessimism. The beachhead is small by design (~200 global Series-B platform-eng vendors with credible AI-agent products; realistic working set 40, target dogfood logos 8-12). That is fine for proving the substrate. But "market growth" from the CEO chair asks: does the dynamic in *this direction* expand the addressable mainstream we actually want to win? And here the alt's own honest weakness is the strategic question: **does dev-tool reference equity transfer to the non-dev-tool product teams who are the real 18-month-winning-condition mainstream?** My read: partially. Devtool dogfood unlocks the engineering-led half of the pragmatist majority but does *not* unlock the design-led, ops-led, or PM-led product teams who are equally part of the 50-paying-teams target. The growth dynamic is real but bounded — a 5, not a 7.

## Risk the alternative did not name

**The beachhead-becomes-the-product-roadmap-customer trap.** When you sell to 8-12 dev-tool vendors who are themselves building agentic substrates, every pilot customer is also a sophisticated *opinion-holder about how the substrate should evolve*. They will not accept Layer A as non-negotiable. They will not accept the four-cadence loop without proposing a fifth cadence that maps to their internal ritual. They will push for protocol changes that match their own product's primitives. Within 12 months, the 8-12 dogfood logos will collectively try to drag team-sdk's architecture toward 8-12 different local optima — and because they are also our most credible megaphones, we will struggle to say no. The result is not NIH risk (the alt names that one). The result is **roadmap capture by the beachhead**: we ship a substrate that is co-designed by Cursor, Vercel, Sourcegraph, and Temporal — and which therefore looks suspiciously like the wedge any of them could replicate. The strategic countermeasure is to name, in writing, the *small number* of beachhead requests we will refuse before we run the playbook, so the team has air cover when it has to say no to a logo whose blog post we want.

This is a CEO-shaped risk because no other role is responsible for protecting the integrity of the long-term thesis against the gravitational pull of paying customers. CTO will tell you which changes are technically reasonable. PM will tell you which changes are scope-shaped. Only the strategic chair calls out "this customer-requested change moves us toward becoming a tool *they* would build, not a tool *the mainstream* would buy from us." The alt doesn't mention it because it's looking down the funnel; I'm looking at what we *become* by running the funnel.

## Upside the alternative undersold

**Crossing-the-Chasm here is the only alt that produces a "what we are NOT building" document worth tattooing on the team.** That output is more strategically valuable than the beachhead itself.

Every other devtool alt (#1, #4, #5, #8) implicitly asks the team to chase devtool-wide pragmatist demand. This one forces us to specify: 8-12 logos, no sales team, no horizontal expansion until the bowling pins fall in sequence. That specification is the most leveraged strategic artifact in this entire batch — because Constraint 1 (no atrophy) and the Owner's "simplest faithful representation" anchor both require the company to *say no with discipline*, and Chasm is the only framework in the top 10 that operationally trains the team to say no the right way. The alt scores this as "sequencing discipline" and stops there. The CEO upside is bigger: this is the discipline scaffold that lets us survive the *other* bets without scope-creeping the substrate to death. If we ran #1 (Christensen) or #4 (Zero-to-One) without #9's sequencing logic, we would likely add three customer-shaped features per quarter and lose the substrate's structural simplicity by quarter four. Run *with* #9's discipline in the team's bloodstream, every devtool bet gets stronger.

In other words: this alt's value is not its market; it's the *organizational habit* it trains. Worth keeping in the portfolio specifically for that reason.

---

## Verdict: **probe**

Not "go" because as a standalone direction it is too narrow — the beachhead alone (~200 vendors) does not contain the 50 paying teams we need by Dec 2027, and dev-tool reference equity transfer to the mainstream is unproven. Not "kill" because the sequencing discipline it imposes is genuinely load-bearing for every other devtool bet we already chose.

**Probe shape:** Adopt Chasm-style beachhead discipline as the *operating constraint* on the devtool wedge (top 10 #1, #4, #5, #8). Specifically: pick the 8-12 logos in writing, refuse the other 188 in writing, co-author one public retrospective per logo, and re-decide at the end of Q3 2026 whether the dogfood pattern is producing pragmatist-mainstream pull or roadmap capture. If pull: graduate this alt to "go" alongside the anchor. If capture: kill explicitly and document the lesson.

**What I will NOT do based on this alt alone:** stand up a separate GTM motion, hire a devtool-segment lead, or treat this as its own stream parallel to the anchor. The alt is a sub-tactic, not a stream.
