## Scores

### Viability — 9/10

I am the role responsible for whether the *team* can execute this direction without breaking, and yes — I would sign off, and I want to sign off this week. Reasons:

- **Dogfooding is free marketing AND a forcing function for product truth.** We are a devtool-shaped team building for devtool-shaped teams. Every `_signals.jsonl` entry we publish about ourselves is simultaneously a sales asset and a product critique. No other segment in the batch lets us run product development and marketing through the same artifact.
- **The buyer is the user.** No procurement, no champion-hunt, no security questionnaire-as-gate. A CTO at a 30-person devtool company can adopt team-sdk in a Friday afternoon and have signal by the next Monday standup. That collapses the entire pre-revenue funnel that kills most B2B SaaS in the first 18 months.
- **Ergonomics already match.** CLI + markdown + git-native is what these teams already live in. We are not asking them to change tools; we are asking them to make their existing tools self-aware. The cognitive cost of adoption is near zero.
- **We can credibly execute with our current shape.** Open-core, lighthouse design partners, conference talks, public dogfooding threads — this is a motion a small team can run without hiring a sales org we cannot yet afford.

I am holding back one point because the "incumbents copy slowly" moat is structurally weaker than classic Christensen low-end disruption assumes (the file admits this) — but for an 18-month winning window, 18 months of clear water is sufficient. We do not need a forever-moat. We need a falsification window.

### Market growth potential — 7/10

Honest. The TAM is narrow — there are not hundreds of thousands of 15-80-person Series-A/B devtool companies. The file flags this directly: "we'd hit the count but on a narrow base." I am scoring this 7 because:

- **Inside the 18-month window, growth potential is high.** We need 50 teams. There are several thousand companies that fit the beachhead description, and we only need 50 of them to commit. The conversion math is friendly.
- **Beyond 18 months, growth potential is uncertain.** The bridge from "devtool companies" to "every product team" is not specified by this alternative alone — that is what chasm (#9) and blue-ocean (#8) are for in the sequencing. A pure Christensen reading does not give us the bowling-pin map.
- **The segment is structurally growing.** Platform engineering, AI-native devtools, observability — all expanding categories with continuous Series A/B formation. We are not betting on a flat market.

I would score 9 if the TAM-bridge were specified. I am not docking it for being narrow today because narrow-and-credible beats wide-and-uncertain when the winning condition is a *count of teams with signal evidence*, not ARR.

## Risk this alternative did not name (CEO lens)

**The strategic-clarity risk: this bet wins so cleanly on the metabolism axis that the team will treat it as "the strategy" rather than "the anchor of a portfolio."** That is a company-shaping mistake.

The file frames this alternative as a beachhead. Triage frames it as "anchor bet" paired with regulated (revenue grounding) and agency (diversification). But once we publicly commit and start publishing devtool dogfooding threads, the gravitational pull of *every* downstream decision — hiring, roadmap, conference circuit, messaging, design partner selection — will be toward devtool. Within two quarters, we will be a devtool-substrate company by accident, having killed the regulated and agency optionality not by deciding to but by failing to defend the calendar against the bet that is working.

This is the classic founder trap that Christensen himself wrote about: *the disrupting wedge consumes the disruptor*. The devtool segment is uniquely sticky for the team's own attention because the feedback loop is so tight and the credibility wins are so visible. We will literally feel smarter every week we work on it. That feeling is the risk.

**What the alternative should have named:** the percentage of CEO and CTO calendar that is reserved for *non-devtool* design partner work even when the devtool flywheel is spinning. Without that reservation, the portfolio becomes a single-segment bet inside one quarter, and we discover the TAM ceiling at month 14 with no second arc warmed up. I would set that reservation at minimum 25% CEO calendar and a named CTO design-partner liaison for regulated and agency from week one — not week 20.

## Upside this alternative undersold (CEO lens)

**The "category creation as recruiting moat" upside is much bigger than the file frames it.**

The file mentions community-led GTM and Hacker News credibility. That is correct but small. What it undersells: *publicly-dogfooded substrate becomes the strongest engineering hiring signal of the decade for a certain kind of senior engineer.* The same engineer who reads our `_signals.jsonl` and thinks "I want to buy this for my team" also reads it and thinks "I want to work for the team that produces this corpus."

The team-sdk substrate is, by design, the *most legible engineering culture artifact a company can publish*. It shows how decisions get made, how dissent is encoded, how learning compounds. For a senior engineer evaluating five offers, that is signal nothing else produces. A devtool company that adopts team-sdk and publishes their loop publicly is using us to recruit talent their competitors cannot reach. The CEOs of those companies know this — and senior-engineer recruiting is a top-three pain point at every Series A/B devtool company I have ever spoken to.

This is not a side benefit. This is a buying motivation the file does not surface. We should be testing the "we make your engineering culture legible to candidates" pitch in week one of the wedge, because if it lands, it shortens every sales conversation by 60% and turns each lighthouse customer into a recruiting partner with a structural reason to stay public.

The upside compounds: lighthouse company A publishes their substrate → senior engineer X joins them partly because of it → engineer X tells engineer Y at company B → company B adopts → company B publishes → repeat. This is a Reed's-Law-shaped flywheel the network-effects alternative (the dropped near-miss) actually pointed at but framed around benchmarks instead of recruiting. Recruiting is the hotter wire.
