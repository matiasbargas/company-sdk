**Synthesized by:** Greg (CEO)

**Inputs:** `_product-vision.md`, `_triage.md` (10 alternatives), team panel results (CEO/CMO/CFO/CRO ×10)

## Executive summary (≤200 words)

The panel's pattern is clear: **the devtool wedge is the only place the 18-month winning condition can actually be falsified**, and the regulated bet is the only place revenue density makes the company fundable. Three alternatives carry that load.

**Recommended top 3 (in order):**

1. **`christensen-disruption__devtool-companies`** — the anchor. Metabolism-native, public dogfood becomes the marketing artifact, lowest CAC, and (the panel's biggest add) the substrate doubles as a senior-engineer **recruiting and category-vocabulary** asset that compounds across every other bet.
2. **`crossing-the-chasm__regulated-enterprise`** — the revenue grounding. One audit-survived Tier-2 BFSI/health-payer logo is the single highest-leverage cross-segment narrative asset in the entire batch. The risk is Big-4 channel capture; the answer is dual-SKU + 30% direct-sales motion baked into channel contract #1.
3. **`lean-startup__devtool-companies`** — the **strategic insurance**. The 90-day MVL with one lighthouse IS the falsification test for the winning condition. Without this running underneath bets 1 and 2, we'll discover the friction-delta signal doesn't generalize at month 14 with nothing warmed up.

**Headline insight:** the devtool segment is not a P&L line — it is a **capitalized customer-acquisition asset** funding the regulated and agency segments. Treat it accordingly.

## Ranked top 3 — deep dive

### #1 — `christensen-disruption__devtool-companies` (ANCHOR BET)

**Verdict:** GO (3 of 4 panel: CEO, CMO, CRO go; CFO probe on monetization).

**Why it ranks #1.** Highest combined viability across the panel (CEO 9, CMO 8, CFO 7, CRO 8) — and the only alternative where all four roles independently surfaced *bigger-than-the-file* upside.

#### Role-by-role view

- **CEO (V9 G7, go):** Christensen wedge with native metabolism fit. The risk only the CEO seat owns: **strategic-clarity capture** — within two quarters the devtool feedback loop will consume hiring, roadmap, and design-partner selection by default, killing regulated/agency optionality not by decision but by calendar drift. Defense requires a **written 25% CEO calendar reservation** + named CTO design-partner liaison for non-devtool segments from week one.
- **CMO (V8 G6, go):** `_signals.jsonl` is the first B2B marketing channel where **the artifact, the proof, and the message are the same file**. Collapses the entire content-marketing function for the beachhead. But: lighthouse abandonment in an open-substrate dogfood model has **narrative-half-life** properties — every public adopter becomes a story we must keep alive, scaling CS attention linearly with target count, currently unbudgeted.
- **CFO (V7 G6, probe):** **Open-core boundary leaks through the OSS itself** — devtool engineers are the cohort most likely to self-host pgvector to bypass a $30K invoice, *because the OSS taught them how*. SOC2 audit export is one-time, not a recurring WTP anchor. The financial model needs a **structurally hard-to-self-host paid layer** (cross-team benchmarks, federated corpus search under NDA, recurring audit re-runs) before unit economics close.
- **CRO (V8 G7, go):** The **lowest-CAC paid-conversion engine in the top 10**. Eight weeks of compounding friction data is automated qualification — the team has self-selected as serious. Comparable OSS devtools (PostHog, Cal.com) convert OSS→paid at 3-5× baseline when the trigger is internal to the substrate. CAC structurally near-zero for first 50-100 paid accounts.

#### Risks not named by the alt author

1. **Identity capture by the wedge itself** (CEO) — devtool gravity drowns the portfolio.
2. **Self-hosting as competitive vector** (CFO) — the OSS we publish teaches the buyer how to bypass us.
3. **Lighthouse-success-protocol gap** (CRO) — community-led GTM has no built-in CS motion to catch a drifting lighthouse before public churn.
4. **Narrative-half-life cost** (CMO) — lighthouse stories require ongoing CS investment that doesn't scale.

#### Upsides undersold by the alt author

1. **Recruiting moat** (CEO + CFO + CRO converge) — public dogfood is the most legible engineering-culture artifact a company can publish. Senior-engineer recruiting CAC drops 25%+ at lighthouses; that's $100-400K/year per logo, more than the seat ACV. The pitch *"we make your engineering culture legible to candidates"* should be tested in week one.
2. **Devtool = capitalized CAC engine for regulated/agency** (CFO) — each lighthouse is a zero-cash-CAC enterprise lead generator for segments with 3-10× higher ACV. Highest-ROI customer acquisition channel in the entire top-10 even at modest standalone ARR.
3. **`_signals.jsonl` as case-study-in-itself** (CMO) — read as engineering ground truth, not vendor-vetted marketing. Pushes true viability closer to 9.5 if abandonment is mitigated.

#### Recommended next step: **COMMIT — but with three guardrails written first**

- Owner-signed **25% CEO calendar non-negotiable** for non-devtool segments (CEO risk mitigation).
- **One lighthouse first**, signal-gated before a second is signed (sequencing discipline borrowed from #3).
- **Day-one paid SKU design** that is structurally hard to self-host (CFO risk mitigation) — cross-team benchmarks or federated corpus search, NOT SOC2 export.

### #3 — `lean-startup__devtool-companies` (STRATEGIC INSURANCE BET)

**Verdict:** mixed (CEO go, CMO/CFO/CRO probe) — but the CEO seat is the right seat to overweight here.

**Why it ranks #3.** Scores look weakest of the three (V5-8 / G5-6) but the strategic role is **load-bearing**: this is the only alternative explicitly designed as the **falsification test for the winning condition itself.** The 90-day MVL — "run one pod, four-cadence loop, measure if `_signals.jsonl` shows the 30% friction-reduction signature" — IS the way the 18-month winning condition gets confirmed or killed. Without it running underneath bets #1 and #2, we discover the signal doesn't generalize at month 14 with no second arc warmed up.

The other devtool alternatives (#4 zero-to-one, #8 blue-ocean, #9 chasm-devtool) are **subsumed into this bet's strategic role**: their best insights (the "observability for cognition" narrative from #4, the recruiting-weapon framing from #8, the bowling-pin sequencing from #9) ride inside the MVL motion rather than running as parallel streams. This is why #3 ranks above all three.

#### Role-by-role view

- **CEO (V8 G6, go):** The substrate's value to US — as **hiring and credibility instrument across all three bets** — may exceed its value to the lighthouse customer inside the 18-month window. Risk: **lighthouse-dependency cascade** — if a lighthouse has a bad quarter for unrelated reasons (layoffs, founder drama), the corpus shows friction not decreasing, and the dev-tools ecosystem reads it as team-sdk failing. **One lighthouse first; signal-gate before the next.**
- **CMO (V5 G6, probe):** **`_signals.jsonl` is not a measurement output — it IS the positioning statement in artifact form**, and makes a category claim no incumbent (Jellyfish, LinearB, DX, Swarmia) can match. They sell engineering velocity; we sell judgment improvement, owned by the team. But: **day-1 public dogfooding with no signal-gating decision before publication is brand-suicidal** if the first curve is flat. Requires a **day-60 min-viable-delta gate** before any artifact is published.
- **CFO (V6 G5, probe):** **Lighthouse spend is a CAC-to-content arbitrage that funds three segments at once** — properly allocated 1/3 R&D, 1/3 brand, 1/3 cross-segment enablement, the per-segment CAC for regulated and agency drops materially because their proof artifacts are pre-paid here. Risk: **BYOC/on-prem hosted-margin trap** strips infra markup while preserving full COGS — hosted ARR at 70%+ silently converts to support ARR at 40-50%.
- **CRO (V6 G5, probe):** A Linear/Vercel-grade lighthouse publishing a documented 30% friction-delta `_signals.jsonl` over two quarters is not just devtool credibility — it is the **single artifact that shortens sales cycles in regulated and agency segments without us being in the room.** Estimated 2-3× cycle-time compression, 1.5-2× close-rate lift elsewhere. Risk: **hosted analytics tier has no defensible billing event specified** — needs SKU sharpness before lighthouses start, or champion installs → zero conversion → full self-host migration at month 4.

#### Risks not named by the alt author

1. **Lighthouse cascade risk on a denominator of 3-5 companies** (CEO) — sequencing constraint is non-negotiable: one lighthouse first, prove publicly, then add.
2. **Day-60 min-viable-delta publication gate missing** (CMO) — file implies day-1 public dogfood with no signal check.
3. **Hidden $300-500K/year developer-relations headcount tax** (CFO) inside what looks like a one-time experiment cost.
4. **Hosted tier billing event undefined** (CRO) — champion install ≠ paid conversion.

#### Upsides undersold by the alt author

1. **Cross-segment proof artifact** — the lighthouse `_signals.jsonl` is the screenshot that closes deals in regulated and agency for 18 months (CMO + CRO converge).
2. **CAC-to-content arbitrage funds three segments at once** (CFO) — every other bet's CAC drops because proof artifacts are pre-paid here.
3. **Recruiting + credibility across all three bets** (CEO) — substrate value to us > substrate value to the lighthouse.

#### Recommended next step: **PROBE — run the MVL on the first devtool lighthouse FROM bet #1**

- **One lighthouse only.** Day-60 gate: minimum viable delta in `_signals.jsonl` before any public artifact ships.
- **SKU sharpness before the contract closes** — billing event named (cross-team benchmark queries, federated corpus search, recurring audit re-runs — not SOC2 export).
- **DevRel headcount budgeted explicitly** ($300-500K/year), not hidden in experiment cost.

## Killed (active kill — kill log entry)

**`jobs-to-be-done__regulated-enterprise`** (panel V5-6 / G8).

**Reason:** The panel gave it the highest goodness scores in the entire batch (G8 unanimous) — but the viability scores (V5-6) and **two converging structural risks** make it the wrong tool for this winning window.

1. **CFO surfaced an un-modeled contingent liability** in the "regulator-ready + indemnification" SKU: tail-risk of regulatory claims (FDA 10y, OCC 5-7y, GDPR Article 22 open-ended) gets tied to every shipped audit packet. Acquirer diligence will discount enterprise value 20-50% or require seller-funded escrow held for the statute period. The file framed indemnification as pricing premium; it is a **balance-sheet liability that destroys company-sale optionality** — and no role in the regulated triad (chasm, disruption, JTBD) flagged it except in this alternative.
2. **CRO surfaced revenue-pull positioning calcification**: "regulator-ready" SKU with indemnification rewrites pricing pages, RFP responses, AE comp plans, and partner enablement around audit-evidence framing within two quarters — locking us out of selling the substrate's actual value because the buyer org puts compliance and product-ops in different cost centers.
3. **CMO surfaced category mis-indexing**: first three reference accounts land us under Gartner AI TRiSM / Forrester AI Governance taxonomies — compared on GRC feature checklists, priced against GRC budgets, renewed on single-event compliance spend. The dissent-and-revise loops (the substrate's actual value) become invisible to the GRC buyer.

These three risks compound: the indemnification SKU is the wedge that locks in the wrong category, which locks in the wrong buyer cost-center, which destroys exit value. **#2 (chasm-regulated) covers this segment without taking any of those risks** because it sells judgment corpus first, audit byproduct second. JTBD-regulated is dominated by chasm-regulated — keeping it warm wastes attention.

## The cross-cutting pattern — what the panel revealed about the vision

**The vision treated the segments as independent paths to the same destination. The panel revealed they are not independent — they are a single coupled portfolio where each segment is a different *kind of asset* for the others.**

Three convergent insights the vision did not name:

1. **Devtool ≠ a P&L line. Devtool = a capitalized customer-acquisition asset.** Every C-suite role independently surfaced this. The CFO named it explicitly ("the devtool segment is not a P&L line — it is a capitalized customer acquisition asset for the regulated and agency segments"). The CRO named the same shape from sales economics ("lighthouse motion is a cross-channel GTM multiplier, estimated 2-3× cycle-time and 1.5-2× close-rate elsewhere"). The CMO named it from narrative ("the dev-tool wedge writes the category vocabulary once; the regulated and agency bets specialize it"). The CEO named it from talent ("the substrate's value to US — as hiring and credibility instrument across all three bets — may exceed its value to the lighthouse customer"). The vision treated bets as parallel; the panel sees them as **funded by devtool and harvested elsewhere**.

2. **`_signals.jsonl` is not a measurement output. It is the positioning statement, the marketing artifact, the sales asset, and the falsification test — all at once.** Four roles surfaced this from four angles. The vision named `_signals.jsonl` as the *measurement* of the winning condition; the panel sees it as the **product itself**. This collapses content marketing, customer references, and proof-of-value into one append-only file. **The implication for the vision:** the winning condition isn't measured on `_signals.jsonl` — `_signals.jsonl` is *how the winning condition is sold*.

3. **The substrate is also a recruiting moat — and that is load-bearing, not decoration.** The CEO, CFO, CMO, and CRO all surfaced this independently across bets #1, #4, #5, and #8. Public dogfood compresses senior-engineer hiring CAC by 25%+ at every lighthouse. At $400K all-in per staff engineer with 4-9 month time-to-fill, this is **a quantifiable margin lever the vision treats as side-effect**. The pricing frame "we charge 25% of your recruiting cost savings" (Blue Ocean panel) reframes the entire ACV ceiling for the devtool segment from $5-30K seat licenses to $50-200K recruiting-budget contracts. **Implication:** the substrate compounds *the team's judgment* (vision-stated) AND *the team's ability to hire the next person who joins that judgment* (vision-unstated). The second compounding loop may be the one that funds the first.
