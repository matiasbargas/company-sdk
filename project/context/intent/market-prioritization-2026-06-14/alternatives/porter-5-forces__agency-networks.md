# Alternative: Porter's Five Forces × Agency networks / product consultancies running multiple client teams

**Framework:** Porter's Five Forces
**Segment:** Agency networks / product consultancies running multiple client teams
**Generated:** 2026-06-14

## The market direction in one sentence

Partners at mid-size product consultancies buy team-sdk as the cross-engagement operating substrate that turns each client team's accumulated judgment into a reusable, defensible asset the agency owns.

## What the framework reveals about this segment

Porter's lens on agency networks shows an industry under structural duress: buyer power is high and rising (clients demand fixed-bid outcomes and increasingly run their own AI pilots), supplier power is shifting from people to model vendors (foundation labs capture margin that used to flow to senior practitioners), threat of substitutes is acute (in-house AI teams, fractional CTOs, vertical SaaS), threat of new entrants is the highest in a decade (any solo operator with a strong agent stack can underbid a 20-person practice), and intra-industry rivalry compresses margins on every RFP. The structural escape valve is **proprietary methodology that compounds across engagements** — the one asset that does not commoditize when models do. Agencies that codify how they think win on retention and price; agencies that do not lose to the next entrant with a cheaper rate card.

## The team-sdk value proposition for this segment, via this framework

team-sdk is positioned as the agency's **methodology vault and engagement substrate**: Layer A holds the firm's non-negotiable craft constraints and Soul (the partnership's brand-defining judgment), Layer B is per-client (multi-tenant by design — every engagement is its own corpus), and the four-cadence loop runs across every active client team simultaneously. Section 32 Critique-Revise and Red-Team loops become the agency's quality moat — work shipped to clients has been adversarially examined by the firm's own encoded standards. The Halloway Ratchet doctrine prevents junior consultants from regressing senior decisions across handoffs. Cross-engagement pattern extraction (anonymized signals from many Layer Bs feeding partner-level retros) is the compounding asset that defends against new entrants: a two-year-old agency on team-sdk has institutional memory a solo operator cannot replicate at any price.

## GTM motion implied

**Partner-led, consultative sales with a land-and-expand inside each agency network.** Porter's lens demands selling to the decision-maker who feels the structural pressure — the practice lead or managing partner whose margin is being squeezed, not the engineering director. Motion: direct outreach to top-100 product consultancies (Thoughtworks-tier down to 30-person shops), partner-level diagnostic conversation ("show me your last three engagements' retro outputs"), 90-day pilot on one practice line, expansion across practices. Channel partnerships with the agency-network associations (Bureau of Digital, SoDA) accelerate distribution.

## Monetization shape

Hybrid: per-active-client-engagement license (the unit of value) plus an annual platform fee tied to partner seats and methodology-vault access.

## Honest weaknesses of this alternative

The framework-segment combination is genuinely strong but has one structural awkwardness: Porter's lens optimizes for **defensible competitive position**, which pushes agencies to treat the substrate as a moat — but team-sdk's Constraint 1 (human agency, no atrophy) and Soul principle "diversity is our superpower" resist becoming a lock-in tool. There is a real tension between selling "your methodology, codified and defensible" and the open-substrate ethos. Agencies may also resist Layer A standardization across their own practices (each partner thinks their craft is special), making rollout politically slow even when the ROI math is obvious.
