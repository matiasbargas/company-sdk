# Alternative: Christensen's Innovator's Dilemma × Regulated enterprises (BFSI, healthcare, govtech, pharma)

**Framework:** Christensen's Innovator's Dilemma
**Segment:** Regulated enterprises (BFSI, healthcare, govtech, pharma)
**Generated:** 2026-06-14

## The market direction in one sentence

Internal AI/ML and data-platform teams inside regulated enterprises adopt team-sdk as the audit-grade operating substrate their GRC platform refuses to instrument, because the work moves faster than control catalogs can describe it.

## What the framework reveals about this segment

Christensen's lens reframes the segment away from "sell GRC to compliance officers" — that is sustaining-innovation territory, where Archer, ServiceNow GRC, OneTrust, AuditBoard, and the Big 4 already overshoot. The overshoot is in *enterprise-wide control taxonomy depth*: SOX, HIPAA, GxP, FedRAMP, DORA, EU AI Act mapped exhaustively against a static control library. What incumbents structurally *cannot* serve is the **team-level epistemic trail**: why a data scientist chose this feature set, which red-team challenge killed the prior model, what the EM decided on Tuesday. That work is happening *now*, mostly in Slack, Notion, and Jupyter — invisible to GRC. It is **non-consumption** of audit-grade context tooling. The new-market disruption foothold is exactly there: serve the AI/ML pod, the platform squad, the clinical-data team — under the radar of the CISO's GRC budget — with a substrate whose Layer B append-only provenance and §32 Critique-Revise ledger *happen to satisfy* EU AI Act Article 12 logging, FDA SaMD GMLP, FFIEC model-risk governance, and DORA ICT-risk evidence requirements. Incumbents will not respond because the deal sizes are too small, the buyer is the wrong title, and the artifact is "developer tooling," not GRC.

## The team-sdk value proposition for this segment, via this framework

The configuration that lands: Layer B as the **canonical model/feature/decision provenance ledger**, with §15a BU lead routing mapped to regulatory domains (CLO for privacy, CISO for security, plus a project-specific MRM lead for model risk). §32 Red-Team and Critique-Revise loops produce auditor-ready challenge records by default. The Halloway Ratchet (§24) becomes the doctrinal record for irreversible model-deployment decisions. Loop and sprint cadences are tuned to align with quarterly model-risk attestations and continuous-monitoring obligations. Crucially: the team-sdk substrate does not *replace* the GRC platform — it *feeds* it, exporting evidence packs the GRC tool can ingest. team-sdk wins the team's daily loop; the GRC platform keeps the enterprise control register.

## GTM motion implied

**Bottom-up, developer/MLOps-led, with a compliance-evidence wedge.** Christensen's disruption requires entering below the radar of the incumbent's account team. Sell to the head of ML platform or the head of data engineering — titles the GRC vendor's enterprise rep does not call on. Open-source the core SDK and the evidence-export adapters (so security review is trivial). Land via a single regulated pod, expand horizontally across pods, then surface upward only when the CISO discovers the substrate is already producing the audit evidence they were paying a Big-4 firm to assemble. Partner with model-risk-management consultancies for last-mile mapping to specific frameworks.

## Monetization shape

Hybrid: open-source core + paid per-team evidence-export and framework-mapping packs (EU AI Act, FFIEC SR 11-7, FDA GMLP, HIPAA, DORA), plus a per-seat enterprise tier once usage crosses the GRC threshold.

## Honest weaknesses of this alternative

The combination is awkward in a specific way: Christensen predicts disruptors win because incumbents *cannot* move down-market due to margin structure — but team-sdk's natural buyer here (the ML platform lead) is *not* the budget holder for compliance, and the compliance budget holder *will* eventually notice and either co-opt or block. The 6-18 month sales cycle the segment is known for is not the disruption cycle; it is the *sustaining* cycle, and once team-sdk surfaces upward it must survive a procurement gauntlet (SOC 2 Type II, vendor risk, data-residency, FedRAMP for govtech) that disruptive entrants rarely survive intact. There is also a real risk that "AI governance" incumbents (Credo AI, Holistic AI, Fairly) move *down* into the team workflow before team-sdk moves *up* into compliance — they are closer to that boundary than ServiceNow is. Finally, Layer B's append-only provenance is a *gift* to regulated work but a *liability* in jurisdictions with right-to-erasure obligations on personnel-attributable records; this needs an architectural answer before the first healthcare or EU deal.
