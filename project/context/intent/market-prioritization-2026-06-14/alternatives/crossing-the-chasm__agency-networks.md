# Alternative: Crossing the Chasm × Agency networks / product consultancies running multiple client teams

**Framework:** Crossing the Chasm
**Segment:** Agency networks / product consultancies running multiple client teams
**Generated:** 2026-06-14

## The market direction in one sentence

Practice leads at mid-size product consultancies (8-25 client pods) buy team-sdk to make pod judgment portable across engagements, so client-team kickoffs collapse from weeks to days.

## What the framework reveals about this segment

Crossing the Chasm forces us to name a single beachhead — not "agencies" but the specific niche whose pain is acute enough to adopt early and visible enough to pull pragmatists across. Inside agency networks, the beachhead is **mid-size product consultancies running 8-25 simultaneous client pods where partners feel the "every engagement starts from zero" tax in billable terms every Monday**. Their pain is not abstract — it is the partner watching a senior engineer re-derive a fintech KYC pattern they shipped at another client six months ago, because no substrate held it. Innovators in this niche (the 1-2 forward consultancies who already maintain internal playbooks in Notion / Confluence) become loud reference customers because their economics improve measurably within a quarter. Their case studies — "we cut engagement ramp from 14 days to 3" — are the chasm-crossing artifact. Pragmatist agencies (the McKinsey Digital / Slalom / Thoughtworks-shape buyers) follow only when 3-5 named visionary firms have publicly attributed margin to the substrate.

## The team-sdk value proposition for this segment, via this framework

The wedge configuration is **Layer A as the firm's house doctrine (Soul + Constraints stay constant across clients), Layer B as the per-client engagement corpus (constraints, domains, decisions live with that client), Layer C scratched per mission**. The Halloway Ratchet (§24) becomes the firm's "we never make the same mistake at two clients" guarantee. The Bus protocol gives partners a single readable artifact across all active engagements. Section 32 Red-Team loops become the firm's differentiating quality signal sold to clients. Critically, the four-cadence metabolism maps onto the agency's existing rhythm (engagement kickoff = loop, weekly partner review = daily, sprint review = sprint, quarterly partner meeting = quarterly) — the substrate disappears into the firm's existing operating model rather than fighting it.

## GTM motion implied

**Partner-led, anchored on 5-8 visionary consultancies named by hand.** Crossing the Chasm prescribes a "whole product" focus on a tightly defined beachhead — which here means white-glove onboarding of the first cohort, co-authoring their Layer A doctrine with them, and producing the public case studies that pull pragmatists across. Bottom-up product-led growth fails here because the buyer is the partner / practice lead, not the IC engineer; the IC has no authority to standardize a substrate across client pods. Sales motion is reference-driven: each visionary customer generates 2-3 pragmatist intros.

## Monetization shape

Hybrid: per-firm license floor + per-active-engagement seat tier (scales with client pod count, aligns price to value).

## Honest weaknesses of this alternative

The combination is awkward in one specific way: **Crossing the Chasm assumes a single product crossing into a single mainstream market, but agency networks are themselves a vertical inside the larger "product teams" TAM**. Treating agencies as the beachhead may trap team-sdk in a niche where the chasm-crossing reference customers are not credible to in-house product teams (which is the larger prize). Agencies also have a structural incentive to keep their substrate proprietary — they may fork rather than adopt, undermining the network-effect thesis. And the multi-tenant separation requirements (one client's Layer B must never leak into another's) add product complexity that pure in-house buyers do not impose. The beachhead may be real but small, with the bridge to the next segment harder than Moore's model suggests.
