## The market direction in one sentence

Partners at multi-client product consultancies buy team-sdk to ramp a new pod into client-context fluency in days instead of weeks, so billable hours go to judgment, not rediscovery.

## What the framework reveals about this segment

The JTBD lens cuts through the noisy "agency tooling" category by naming the actual job being hired: **"When I open a new client engagement (or rotate staff on an existing one), help me make the receiving pod sound institutionally competent on day three, not week three, so the client never feels they're paying us to learn their business."** The current hires for this job — Notion wikis, Loom walkthroughs, ramp buddies, "shadow the senior" weeks — fail because they are passive corpora the new pod must mine, not active substrates that reason alongside them. The functional job is context transfer; the emotional job is partner anxiety relief (no more "is this pod ready to be in front of the CFO yet?"); the social job is the client perceiving the agency as a coherent institution rather than a rotating cast of strangers. Agency P&L makes the ROI math trivial: every day shaved off ramp is a day at the realized billable rate, and every pod that sounds fluent on day three protects renewal economics.

## The team-sdk value proposition for this segment, via this framework

The exact configuration that wins: **Layer B as the client substrate** — each client engagement gets its own Layer B corpus (domain constraints, regulatory posture, stakeholder map, prior decision log, vetoed approaches), and the Section 18 BU lead routing means a new pod's questions ("why did we reject the event-sourcing approach in Q1?") get answered by the corpus, not by interrupting the partner. The Section 24 Halloway Ratchet enforces that hard-won client-specific judgment never regresses across staff rotations. The four-cadence metabolism becomes the standard delivery rhythm sold to clients as "continuous discovery built in." Critically, Layer A (Constraints + Soul) stays the agency's, while Layer B is per-client and portable to the client at engagement end — turning the substrate from a lock-in risk into a deliverable.

## GTM motion implied

**Partner-led, anchored by a flagship network.** Agency partners buy from agency partners; the decision-maker is a practice lead who needs proof from a peer firm before signing. Land one mid-tier consultancy (50-200 people, 8-15 active engagements), instrument ramp-time and rediscovery hours, publish the case study, and let the partner WhatsApp network do the rest. JTBD fits this motion because the job is universal across firms but the proof is specific — one credible ramp-time delta is worth ten generic decks. Layer onto industry events (Bureau of Digital, SoDA) for community-led pull.

## Monetization shape

Hybrid: per-active-engagement license (the unit of value) + seat tier inside each engagement + premium for the Layer B export-to-client deliverable.

## Honest weaknesses of this alternative

The JTBD × agency combination is genuinely tight on the job side but awkward on three fronts. First, agencies are notoriously bad buyers of internal infrastructure — they bill for client work, not for sharpening their own saw, and "substrate adoption" competes with billable hours for partner attention. Second, the multi-tenant requirement (strict client-data isolation, often contractually enforced) is real engineering work team-sdk does not currently solve out of the box, and getting it wrong loses the segment permanently. Third, the segment is structurally shrinking in the AI era — many agencies are themselves being disrupted by AI-native competitors, so we may be selling ramp-time relief to a category that is consolidating. The JTBD lens names a real job, but the segment hiring for it may not be the one growing fastest.
