# Alternative: Lean Startup × Agency networks / product consultancies running multiple client teams

**Framework:** Lean Startup
**Segment:** Agency networks / product consultancies running multiple client teams
**Generated:** 2026-06-14

## The market direction in one sentence

Practice leads at product consultancies adopt team-sdk as the cross-client substrate so each engagement's accumulated judgment compounds into the next pitch instead of evaporating with the team.

## What the framework reveals about this segment

Lean Startup asks: what is the riskiest assumption, and what is the cheapest experiment that resolves it? For agencies, the riskiest assumption is **not** "will partners pay" — they already pay for Notion, Linear, Confluence, and bespoke playbooks. The riskiest assumption is whether a context substrate built for *one team representing itself* survives the multi-tenant reality where the team-of-record changes every 8-14 weeks and the context belongs partially to the client. The MVL experiment is narrow: instrument 3 paid engagements at one mid-size consultancy, measure whether `_signals.jsonl` friction-reduction holds when the human team rotates mid-release, and whether the corpus is portable enough to hand to the client at engagement end without leaking other clients' Layer B. If both hold, the pivot/persevere call is clear; if either fails, the segment is structurally wrong for the substrate as designed.

## The team-sdk value proposition for this segment, via this framework

The minimum lovable configuration is Layer A (Constraints + Soul) shared across the practice, Layer B partitioned per client engagement with cryptographic tenancy, and Section 32 Critique-Revise loops tuned for **pitch-to-kickoff knowledge transfer** — the moment that destroys margin at every agency. The four cadences collapse to two that matter here: the per-engagement loop (daily) and the cross-engagement ritual (weekly practice review where partners harvest Layer B patterns into Layer A doctrine). Section 18 context requests become the formal mechanism for "what did the Acme team learn that the Globex team needs?" — the question every practice lead asks and no current tool answers.

## GTM motion implied

**Partner-led, design-partner-first.** Lean Startup discipline says: do not scale until the loop is validated. Recruit 3 design-partner consultancies (50-200 people each), embed for one quarter at zero cost in exchange for instrumentation rights, and only open self-serve once the friction-reduction curve is reproducible across all three. Partners are the buyers; practice leads are the champions; senior consultants are the daily users. Community comes later — first prove the loop.

## Monetization shape

Hybrid: per-practice license floor + per-active-engagement usage tier (engagements, not seats, are the unit of value).

## Honest weaknesses of this alternative

The combination is awkward in two specific places. First, Lean Startup's build-measure-learn cadence assumes one product team iterating on one product; agencies iterate on N products simultaneously with N different clients, so "the experiment" is plural and the learning signal is noisy. Second, agency margin pressure means the substrate must produce billable-hour ROI inside the first engagement, which fights team-sdk's "compounds over quarters" thesis — the long-game value proposition is structurally mismatched to short-engagement economics. The honest read: this is a real market, but the framework's solo-startup origin makes the experiment design harder than it looks.
