# Alternative: Lean Startup × AI labs / research orgs

**Framework:** Lean Startup
**Segment:** AI labs / research orgs
**Generated:** 2026-06-14

## The market direction in one sentence

Frontier and applied AI research orgs adopt team-sdk as the provenance-bearing substrate for multi-agent research workflows, so every claim, ablation, and review trail compounds into the lab's auditable corpus.

## What the framework reveals about this segment

Lean Startup forces a single question: what is the riskiest assumption, and what is the cheapest experiment that resolves it? For AI labs, the riskiest assumption is NOT "do they need orchestration" — they already build it. It is "will a lab let an external substrate own the provenance trail of their research process, when that trail IS their epistemic credibility?" The minimum-viable-learning experiment is therefore not a product sale; it is a 6-week embedded pilot inside one research pod (3-5 researchers) where team-sdk's Section 32 Critique-Revise and Red-Team loops become the substrate for one open-ended research thread, with the `_signals.jsonl` corpus exported back to the lab as their own artifact. The validated learning is binary: did the pod's peer-review cadence get faster AND more rigorous, or not? Everything else is vanity metrics.

## The team-sdk value proposition for this segment, via this framework

Labs already have model orchestration; what they lack is **a substrate for the human+agent epistemic loop around the models**. team-sdk's Section 32 Critique-Revise + Red-Team loops, Section 24 Halloway Ratchet (irreversibility doctrine — perfect for "do we publish this claim?"), Layer B project domain context (per-research-thread evidence-shaped corpus), and the Bus + BU lead routing become the lab's shared reasoning infrastructure. The four-cadence metabolism maps cleanly onto research rhythms: loop = single experiment, daily = standup, sprint = paper-section iteration, quarterly = research direction review. The lens-specific config: ship a `lab-research` squad with Researcher, Reviewer, Red-Teamer, Provenance Steward, and an Ethics agent — all routing through Coordinator with mandatory Halloway gates before any external claim.

## GTM motion implied

**Community-led, with embedded-pilot conversion.** Lean Startup demands cheap loops; community-led is the cheapest distribution into a segment that publishes openly and trusts peer signal over sales decks. The motion: open-source the `lab-research` squad, publish a paper with one friendly lab (e.g., an independent alignment org) co-authored on the substrate itself, then convert via 6-week embedded pilots run by a founding researcher who lives inside the pod. This fits the segment (prestige-driven, peer-validated, may contribute substrate evolution back) and the framework (each pilot IS a build-measure-learn loop with a binary outcome).

## Monetization shape

Hybrid: substrate is OSS + free; paid tier is **provenance-as-a-service** (signed, exportable, audit-grade `_signals.jsonl` archives) plus per-seat for proprietary lab features (private squads, custom Layer A constraints).

## Honest weaknesses of this alternative

Lean Startup is awkward here because AI labs are NOT a typical "early-customer-discovery" segment — they are sophisticated buyers with strong NIH instincts who will fork before they adopt, and the "moderate willingness to pay" segment note means the monetization ceiling is low even at full penetration. The riskiest assumption (provenance trust) may also be unfalsifiable in 6 weeks — labs grant trust over years, not sprints, so the MVL loop may report a false positive ("pod liked it") that does not translate to org adoption. There is also a strategic risk: labs that DO contribute back may dilute team-sdk's coherence with research-specific patterns that do not generalize to the 50-team winning condition.
