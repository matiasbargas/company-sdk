# Alternative: Aggregation Theory (Thompson) × Educational institutions / bootcamps (CS programs, product schools)

**Framework:** Aggregation Theory (Thompson)
**Segment:** Educational institutions / bootcamps (CS programs, product schools)
**Generated:** 2026-06-14

## The market direction in one sentence

team-sdk becomes the substrate every product school standardizes on so that graduates arrive at hiring companies already fluent in one self-aware team-context corpus — aggregating the demand of hiring teams by owning the relationship with the learner.

## What the framework reveals about this segment

Aggregation Theory says you win by owning the end-user relationship at zero marginal cost, then modularizing the upstream suppliers who used to control distribution. In edu, the "end user" team-sdk should aggregate is not the bootcamp itself — it is the **graduate**, and through the graduate, the **hiring team** that absorbs them. Bootcamps are not the demand; they are the upstream supplier of trained teammates, exactly the layer the aggregator modularizes. If team-sdk becomes the substrate every CS program and product school teaches loops on, every cohort enters the workforce already speaking Bus protocol, BU routing, `_signals.jsonl`, and Layer A/B/C — and hiring companies adopt team-sdk not because we sold it, but because their new hires expect it. The bootcamps become interchangeable training pipelines for a substrate the aggregator owns the relationship with.

## The team-sdk value proposition for this segment, via this framework

The configuration that lands hardest: a **free, education-licensed team-sdk distribution** with pre-built cohort projects ("ship a v2026.Q3.1 release in 6 weeks"), the Section 32 Critique-Revise and Red-Team loops as the core pedagogical instrument (students learn engineering judgment by watching agents dissent), and a graduation artifact — the student's `_signals.jsonl` and `history.md` from their capstone — that they bring to job interviews as proof of how they reason. Layer A constraints become the curriculum's ethics module. The four-cadence substrate becomes how the cohort itself operates. The aggregation effect kicks in when the third cohort graduates: hiring managers start filtering for "team-sdk fluent" in JDs.

## GTM motion implied

**Community-led, with institutional design partners.** Aggregation Theory motions are never sales-led — they require zero-friction onboarding and viral end-user pull. The motion: 3-5 lighthouse bootcamps (Lambda-tier, Reforge, On Deck-style product schools) co-design the curriculum integration in exchange for naming rights and an alumni network feature; instructor evangelists distribute it to peer programs; the substrate goes free-for-edu forever and monetizes downstream where the graduates land. Brand value compounds with each cohort. This fits the framework because the bootcamp is the modularized supplier, not the customer.

## Monetization shape

**Hybrid — free for education, seat-based at downstream employers.** Edu license is $0; revenue is captured at the hiring company where graduates pull adoption.

## Honest weaknesses of this alternative

The 18-month winning condition demands 50 *product teams* with measurable `_signals.jsonl` friction reduction — bootcamp cohorts are not product teams in that sense, and their `_signals.jsonl` will show learning artifacts (lots of corrections, low signal-to-action rate) that look like substrate failure under the success metric. The downstream monetization assumption requires 18-36 months of cohort throughput before any revenue lands, which is longer than the 18-month winning window. Also: bootcamps have notoriously volatile business models (Lambda, 2U, edX collapses) and cannot be counted on as durable distribution. The "graduates pull adoption at employers" mechanic is empirically rare — most graduates conform to their employer's stack, not vice versa. This combo is intellectually clean but operationally slow.
