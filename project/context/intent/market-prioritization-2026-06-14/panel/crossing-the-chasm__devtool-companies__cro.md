# CRO panel score — crossing-the-chasm__devtool-companies

**Role:** CRO (revenue model, GTM motion fit, pricing power, deal velocity, expansion shape, sales-team scalability)
**Date:** 2026-06-14

## Scoring

- **Viability (0-10): 6** — Would sign off on a bounded 90-day probe, not on full execution. Deal velocity and a real content-flywheel upside are CRO-attractive, but pricing power against this buyer is structurally weak and the founder-to-repeatable-sales transition is unaddressed.
- **Market growth (0-10): 5** — Beachhead is ~40 named targets (small). Per-team seat floor caps account ACV against 15-50 person buyers. The "dev-tool refs transfer to non-dev-tool pragmatists" assumption is the strategic gate for years 2-3 and remains unproven. CRO domain reads this as weak-to-unknown on growth shape.

## Risk not named (CRO-shaped)

**The founder-led → repeatable-sales transition gap.** The file frames "no sales team in year one" as discipline, but Crossing-the-Chasm bowling-pin sequencing demands 8-12 lighthouse logos by month 12 — which means deals #13-50 land in months 12-18, exactly when founders must hand off or scale themselves into unrecoverable saturation. There is no plan for who runs deal #13. The CRO-shaped failure mode: founders close 12 lighthouse deals, become the bottleneck running every demo personally, the bowling pins never roll because the founders are saturated, and the chasm doesn't get crossed even though the beachhead succeeded. This is distinct from the NIH risk the file names — it is a sales-system risk, not a buyer-behavior risk.

## Upside undersold (CRO-shaped)

**The co-authored public retrospective as a self-replicating distribution flywheel.** The file names co-authored retros as a wedge tactic. The CRO view: each retro is an evergreen, SEO-durable, founder-network-amplifying asset that generates inbound demo requests for 18+ months. Eight to twelve well-placed retros from named Series-B dev-tool brands could collapse outbound CAC to near-zero by month 9 — a rare case where the GTM motion produces its own distribution. This is structural CAC advantage the file undersells as "tactic."

## Verdict

**Probe.** Run a 90-day, 2-3 pilot test that measures whether the co-authored-retro flywheel actually generates inbound. If retro #1 produces N inbound demo requests inside 60 days, this becomes a go and the founder-handoff plan becomes the next gating question. If it doesn't, the segment's pricing-power and account-size problems dominate and this drops below the regulated triad on revenue grounding.
