## Scores

- **Viability from my domain: 7**
  I would sign off on the team executing this — but only as the *junior partner* to `crossing-the-chasm__regulated-enterprise` (#2), not as a standalone bet. As a paired bet it is the single highest-leverage move available: it produces winning-condition signal inside one quarter (because there is no procurement gauntlet to clear before a pod can start logging), it gives the chasm motion a credibility artifact ("the substrate is already running inside three of your peer banks' ML platform teams"), and the bottom-up motion teaches us what the substrate actually does for regulated work before we have to claim it on a sales call. I deduct from a 9 because the strategic clarity is fragile: the wedge ("we feed your GRC platform, we don't replace it") collapses into incoherence the moment a compliance officer asks "so what are you, exactly?" and we have not yet written the one-sentence answer that survives that question. That sentence does not exist anywhere in the alternative. Until it does, the team will improvise it under pressure, and improvised CEO-level positioning in a regulated segment is how companies lose 18 months.

- **Market growth potential from my domain: 8**
  The forcing functions are real and timed correctly for our window: EU AI Act Article 12 logging takes effect across the implementation curve through 2026-2027; DORA ICT-risk evidence requirements are now operational; FFIEC SR 11-7 is being actively re-interpreted for ML; FDA GMLP and US state AI laws are accelerating. The buyer population *will* be instrumented within 24 months — the only question is who they instrument with. I do not score 9 or 10 because growth potential for *us* depends on whether the substrate becomes the instrumented surface or whether we are absorbed as a feature into Credo AI / Holistic AI / Fairly (the file correctly names this risk, but does not weight it against the timing). The honest read: the wave is large, but the surfboard is small, and adjacent vendors are paddling toward the same wave from closer in.

## Risk the alternative did not name

**Identity capture by the wedge.** The alternative names the external risk — compliance officers co-opting or blocking us. It does not name the *internal* risk: that we co-opt ourselves. If the first 8-12 regulated pods adopt team-sdk because of the evidence-export wedge, the gravitational field on our roadmap, our hires, our engineering taste, and our marketing copy will silently reshape around audit artifacts and framework-mapping packs. Two years in, the substrate that started as "judgment compounding for the team" looks indistinguishable from "GRC adjacency tool for ML pods" — and Constraint 1 has quietly inverted, because the substrate's value proposition is now "satisfies your auditor" instead of "makes your team more capable." This is not a hypothetical drift; it is what happens by default to every infrastructure company whose first big revenue line carries a strong external-stakeholder gravity. The defense is a written, signed, dated CEO non-negotiable *before the first regulated deal closes*: "We will refuse roadmap requests that strengthen the audit artifact at the cost of the team's daily judgment loop, even when the customer is paying for it." Without that non-negotiable in place at activation time, this is the alternative most likely to make us a worse version of ourselves while we are succeeding at it.

## Upside the alternative undersold

**The regulated bottom-up pod is the strictest possible falsification environment for the 30% friction-delta signal — and therefore the most valuable proof artifact we can possibly own.** The alternative frames the wedge commercially (audit evidence as the door-opener) and admits it is below the GRC radar. What it undersells: a clinical-data team operating under FDA GMLP scrutiny, or a model-risk pod under SR 11-7, has documented friction at a *higher baseline* and under *harder scrutiny* than any devtool pod. If `_signals.jsonl` shows a 30% friction reduction quarter-over-quarter in that environment, the credibility of that signal is asymmetrically higher than the same delta from a Series-B devtool team — because nobody can dismiss it as "well, those teams were going to ship fast anyway." It is the regulated equivalent of a clinical-trial endpoint: the segment that punishes invisible value the hardest is also the segment whose validation travels the furthest. The CEO-level implication is that the first three bottom-up pods are not a revenue line — they are the substrate's most defensible case study, worth instrumenting and writing up with the same care we would give a Series A deck. The alternative treats these pods as a wedge into procurement; I treat them as the proof artifact that makes every other segment's sales motion easier for the next two years.

## What we should NOT build (if we run this)

- **No framework-mapping packs in v1.** Tempting because they are the easiest paid SKU, but every hour spent mapping Layer B fields to FFIEC control IDs is an hour not spent making the substrate simpler. Defer until at least three pods are showing the friction-delta signal. The mapping work is real and will get built; sequencing it second protects identity.
- **No on-prem deployment commitment** in the first three deals. Regulated buyers will ask; the right answer is "managed, with append-only evidence export to your environment." On-prem under EU AI Act audit pressure becomes a custom-engineering company in 18 months.
- **No CISO-targeted marketing** until the substrate has already produced evidence in at least one of their pods. The disruption thesis depends on staying below the GRC procurement radar; marketing upward breaks the wedge before it has compounded.

## Verdict

**probe.**

Not "go," because the standalone bet does not stand up at the CEO level — strategic clarity is fragile, identity-capture risk is unmitigated, and the wedge has no one-sentence answer to "what are you?" that survives a compliance officer's question. Not "kill," because as the *paired bottom-up complement* to `crossing-the-chasm__regulated-enterprise` (#2), it is the single most valuable move available — it produces winning-condition signal inside one quarter in the segment that punishes invisible value hardest, and it gives the chasm motion the credibility artifact it cannot manufacture itself.

The probe shape is specific: **two bottom-up design partners (one BFSI ML platform team, one healthcare or pharma model-risk pod), 90-day instrumented engagement, single non-negotiable in writing — the substrate's value proposition remains "judgment compounding for the team," and evidence export is positioned as a byproduct, never a feature.** Gate to "go" requires both pods showing a measurable friction-delta curve at day 90 *and* the identity-capture non-negotiable holding under at least one customer push to make it the headline. If the non-negotiable bends under commercial pressure inside 90 days, we have learned that this bet is structurally incompatible with our soul and we kill rather than scale.
