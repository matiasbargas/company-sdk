# CRO panel review — jobs-to-be-done__agency-networks

**Role:** CRO
**Date:** 2026-06-14
**Mode:** Consultation (deep-score)

## Scores

- **Viability (CRO lens): 7/10**
- **Market growth potential (CRO lens): 6/10**

## CRO reasoning

### Revenue model fit
Per-active-engagement licensing aligns the billing unit with the value unit. Agencies already think in engagement-shaped P&L; they will not resist this geometry the way SaaS-native buyers resist non-seat models. Strong fit.

### Pricing power (underestimated by alternative)
Realized billable rates at mid-tier consultancies sit at ~$200-350/hr. A 2-day ramp-time reduction × 4-6 staff rotations per engagement = $15-25K of margin recovered per engagement. A $8-15K per-engagement ACV is trivial against that — there is materially more pricing headroom than the hybrid model assumes. We are leaving money on the table at the prices the alternative implies.

### Deal velocity
Partner-led GTM is well-understood, but agency procurement for *internal infrastructure* (not client-facing services) runs 90-180 days minimum. This fits 2-3 cycles inside the 18-month window — workable, but tighter than triage acknowledged when it labeled this the "conversion-velocity bet."

### Expansion shape (the quietly exceptional part)
This is what the alternative's author and triage both undersold. Per-engagement licensing means **expansion is automatic with the agency's own growth**. A consultancy growing from 10 to 20 active engagements doubles ARR with zero sales motion, zero seat expansion, zero feature upsell. NRR can structurally land at 130-160% without any land-and-expand playbook. Combined with Layer B export as a margin-expanding *premium SKU* (not just a feature), this is one of the few top-10 segments where steady-state ACV grows at the pace of the client's business — not at the pace of our sales team.

### Sales-team scalability
Partner-led peer referrals scale by trust capital, not headcount. Two outcomes: (a) extraordinary capital efficiency if it works (single-digit-month CAC payback); (b) hard TAM ceiling at ~80-120 agency logos globally matching the ICP, after which each new logo costs 3-5x. Bounded TAM caps scaled revenue but does not invalidate the bet at our stage.

### GTM motion — half-right assumption
Agency partners do refer to peer agency partners. But they refer for *client-facing* services more readily than for *competitive infrastructure*. Once a partner finds something that gives them a ramp-time edge, the proprietary-advantage instinct kicks in and the peer-network referral slows. We should not budget peer referral as if it were SaaS PLG.

## Risk not named (CRO-specific)
**The Layer B export-to-client deliverable is a revenue cannibalization vector.** The alternative celebrates Layer B exportability as Constraint-1-friendly (turning the substrate from lock-in into a deliverable). But if the substrate is exportable to the client AS A DELIVERABLE at engagement end, the client may demand to license the substrate directly — collapsing the per-engagement renewal economics on that engagement and routing future revenue around the agency entirely. The premium-SKU framing of the export only works if export rights are contractually scoped to read-only artifacts, not a live working substrate. Without that contract discipline baked in before first sale, every engagement closeout is a potential ARR leak. This is a 2-week legal sprint to design correctly, but skipping it would be expensive.

## Upside undersold (CRO-specific)
**Per-engagement licensing yields near-automatic NRR through agency engagement velocity** — a revenue shape the alternative treated as merely "tied to the unit of value." Mid-tier consultancies that grow from 10 to 20 active engagements double ARR with zero sales motion. Combined with Layer B export priced as a margin-expanding premium SKU, this is structurally a higher-NRR model than per-seat SaaS for the same ICP. Land 15 mid-tier agencies that grow at agency-industry baseline (8-15% engagement-count growth) and steady-state NRR lands at 130-160% — exceptional for a non-PLG motion. The right framing here is not "convert-the-agency-once" but "ride agency growth as the expansion mechanism."

## Verdict: **PROBE**

The revenue shape is genuinely exceptional and underappreciated by both the alternative's author and the triage report. Pricing power is higher than implied; expansion math is rare. Multi-tenancy engineering and 90-180-day procurement cycles are real gates but not killers. The export-vs-renewal cannibalization risk is fixable with contract design, not strategy.

Worth a single design-partner motion as triage already concludes — but for revenue-model reasons stronger than triage acknowledged. The case for a probe is the NRR shape, not the conversion-velocity story.

## Conditions on probe
1. Multi-tenancy isolation must reach design-partner-grade before first paid engagement (gating dependency, as triage flagged).
2. Layer B export rights contractually scoped before first sale — read-only artifact export, not live substrate transfer. Without this, the per-engagement model leaks.
3. First design partner must be a consultancy with **growth trajectory in engagement count**, not just a stable-revenue shop — otherwise we never observe the NRR expansion that justifies the bet.
4. Track the leading indicator: average ramp days saved per pod rotation. If under 3 days at first design partner, kill — the pricing power thesis collapses below that floor.
